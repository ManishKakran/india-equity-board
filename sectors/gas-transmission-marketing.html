SECTOR OVERVIEW — GAS TRANSMISSION & MARKETING (INDIA)

Industry Background & Latest 2025–26 Data

India's natural gas pipeline sector is at an inflection point driven by three structural tailwinds:

  1. Demand Surge: India's natural gas consumption reached 71.3 BCM in FY2024–25, growing ~6% YoY. The IEA projects a ~60% rise by 2030, reaching 103 BCM/year.
  2. Infrastructure Expansion: As of March 2025, India's authorised natural gas pipeline network stands at 34,233 km. The government has planned an additional 10,805 km expansion, with the grid expected to grow by 50% more by 2030.
  3. Policy Support: PM Modi unveiled a strategic $67 billion investment plan for the Indian gas sector over 5–6 years. The government's target is to raise natural gas's share of India's energy mix to 15% by 2030 (from ~6% today).

City Gas Distribution (CGD) is expected to lead demand growth — CNG stations and residential connections projected to nearly double by 2030. India is the 4th largest LNG importer globally; LNG imports set to more than double by 2030 as domestic production meets only ~50% of demand.

Outside View / Convergence Test (Darwin Framework — Industry Level)

The book asks: "If most companies in the industry can't hack it, why should I believe this company is unique?"

Industry verdict: Gas transmission is a regulated infrastructure utility — not a competitive consumer market. Pipelines are natural monopolies with: - Tariffs fixed by PNGRB (regulator) — revenue is quasi-contracted - Capital-intensive entry barriers (pipeline infrastructure: ₹3–7 crore/km) - Reservation-based / take-or-pay revenue model for pure transmission players - Stable, long-duration demand tied to India's energy transition

Convergence Conclusion: Category (2) — Industry structurally allows returns above cost of capital for participants. Pure transmission (toll-road model) earns stable ROCE. Integrated gas marketing (GAIL model) adds commodity price risk that dilutes ROCE quality. The industry is desirable by structure for pure-play pipeline companies.

Pace of Change: Gas pipelines are slow-changing infrastructure. Technology is mature; disruption risk is low on a 10–15 year horizon. Hydrogen blending is emerging (GAIL pilot, Indore) but a transition threat is 20+ years away. This is exactly the type of slow-changing industry Nalanda favours.

Entity Background

EIT is a SEBI-registered Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT), listed on BSE since March 2019. It owns 100% of Pipeline Infrastructure Ltd. (PIL), which operates India's first bi-directional natural gas pipeline (~1,485 km East-West Pipeline, taken over from Reliance Industries). Sponsor: Rapid Holdings 2 Pte Ltd (indirect subsidiary of Brookfield Corporation, Canada). EnCap Investment Manager Pvt Ltd is the Investment Manager. O&M contractor is a 50:50 JV of the sponsor and Reliance group. By SEBI mandate, the trust distributes at least 90% of net distributable cash flows to unitholders quarterly.

Latest Update

  • Dividend yield reported at ~24.9% (mandatory distribution model)
  • Low interest coverage flagged on Screener.in
  • FCF 5-year and 3-year data unavailable (pass-through trust structure)
  • ROCE 5.47% — far below Nalanda's minimum threshold of 20%

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter / Management Integrity Brookfield Corporation is a large, globally listed private asset manager. No documented fraud or regulatory censure. Private-sector sponsor. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check EIT owns a steady-state operational pipeline asset — not a turnaround story. Revenue is contractual. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.3 — Debt / Leverage InvITs by design carry project-level debt secured against the pipeline assets. Screener explicitly flags low interest coverage. Infrastructure leverage is integral to InvIT economics — D/E in such structures routinely exceeds 0.3. Darwin's book states:

"More than 90 percent of our portfolio companies have — and have always had — excess cash. Only three businesses out of about thirty in our portfolio have some debt. But even this debt is quite small — the maximum debt/equity ratio among these three is 0.3."

InvIT debt structure is fundamentally incompatible with this standard.

Filter 1.3 Verdict: ❌ DISQUALIFY — Debt structure incompatible with Darwin Framework

Additional structural note: InvIT is a pass-through income vehicle, not a compounding operating business. Nalanda's model requires businesses that retain and reinvest capital at high ROCE to compound intrinsic value. An InvIT that mandatorily distributes 90%+ of cash cannot compound in the Darwin sense — it is an income instrument, not a quality compounder.

FRAMEWORK NOTES FOR FUTURE MONITORING

What Would Make Gas Transmission Worth Re-Examining?

The industry structure is attractive (natural monopoly; slow-changing; essential infrastructure; 60% demand growth projected to 2030). The problem is entirely on the ownership and quality side of the current listed universe.

Trigger for re-examination: A privately promoted (non-government), debt-light gas transmission or CGD company listing on Indian exchanges should be screened immediately. The structural tailwinds are real and strong.

Adjacent names worth a fresh Darwin Gate 1 screen (not recommendations): - Torrent Gas — private Torrent Group; promoter-owned CGD play; watch for listing - Adani Total Gas — promoter-owned; but Adani Group's high leverage and conglomerate structure may fail filters 1.3 and 1.4 - Mahanagar Gas (MGL) — verify current ownership split (GAIL + British Gas legacy); if government ownership has reduced, rescreen from scratch

These require independent full Gate 1 analysis before any conclusion.
