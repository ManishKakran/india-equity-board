SECTOR OVERVIEW — INDUSTRY CONTEXT

What This Sector Covers

India's Houseware sector encompasses manufacturers and marketers of: - Pressure cookers & cookware (Hawkins) - Glassware & kitchenware (Borosil, Cello World) - Plastic household goods (Cello World, All Time Plastics, Gothi Plascon) - Moulded furniture & storage (Cello World, All Time Plastics) - Interior products (Maruti Interior) - Commercial kitchen & display equipment (Riddhi Display) - Miscellaneous plasticware (Gorani Industries)

Industry Fundamentals (Outside View / Convergence Test)

Market Size & Growth: India's homeware market was valued at USD 5.56 billion in 2024, projected to reach USD 9.39 billion by 2033 at a CAGR of ~5.93%. The consumer houseware segment (kitchenware) is the largest and fastest-growing category.

Structural Tailwinds: - Rising disposable incomes and expanding middle class (projected 583 million by 2025) - Rapid urbanisation driving modular kitchen demand - Shift from unorganised to organised players (organised penetration still below 30% in many sub-categories) - Premiumisation of kitchenware (consumers upgrading from aluminium to stainless steel, from unbranded to branded) - India — second-largest producer of plastic tableware/kitchenware globally (748,000 tons in 2024)

Structural Headwinds: - Fierce competition from unorganised players operating with lower cost structures - Chinese import competition: average import price of ~$2,804/ton (78% of imports from China), setting a competitive ceiling in commodity segments - Raw material price volatility (polymer/plastic granule prices tied to crude, aluminium and stainless steel cyclicality) - High SKU complexity (thousands of product variants) demanding large working capital

Darwin Convergence Verdict — Industry Outside View:

"If most companies in the industry can't hack it, why should I believe that this company is unique?"

Examining the ROCE of top players in this sector reveals a bifurcated picture: - Hawkins Cookers: Sustained ROCE of 39–46% over 10+ years → structurally attractive business with durable moat - Borosil: ROCE 11–14%, negative FCF → structural capital destruction - Most others: ROCE 8–20%, negative FCF, intense competition

Convergence Conclusion: Category 3 — Only some companies make money. This sector does NOT uniformly allow participants to earn returns above the cost of capital. The ~70–80% unorganised market share, Chinese price competition, and high working capital requirements structurally compress ROCE for most players. Only the undisputed market leaders with strong brand equity and focused product lines can sustain >20% ROCE. This makes individual stock selection — not sector allocation — the critical variable.

COMPANY 1 — CELLO WORLD

CMP: ₹381.95 | P/E: 25.03x | Market Cap: ₹8,437 Cr | ROCE: 17.41%

Business Description

Cello World (listed Nov 2023) operates across three distinct segments: consumer houseware/glassware (65.7% of revenue), writing instruments & stationery (16%), and moulded furniture & allied products (18.4%). It markets products under the "Cello" brand (since 1962 under founder Ghisulal Rathod) and "Unomax" across 15,891 SKUs through 145,000+ retail touchpoints.

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter/Management Integrity The Rathod family (Pradeep, Pankaj, Gaurav) are promoters with 60+ years of family association with the Cello brand. No SEBI/court actions noted. However, a critical red flag: the IPO (Nov 2023) was a 100% Offer for Sale — all ₹1,900 Cr proceeds went to promoters/selling shareholders; the company received nothing. Promoter selling at the peak of their ROCE trajectory (44% at IPO time, now 17%) is a meaningful adverse signal about insider confidence in future returns. Post-IPO, promoter holding has been managed down to comply with minimum public shareholding norms via QIP. Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS — no fraud/regulatory issues, but promoter exit at IPO peak is a concern

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check Not a turnaround story per se — the company has a long operating history. However, the dramatic and sustained ROCE collapse post-IPO (44% → 40.6% FY24 → 22.7% FY25 → 17.41% current) is alarming. This now looks like a company whose exceptional pre-IPO ROCE was partly a function of a lean capital base (negative equity in FY21) that has subsequently been diluted by heavy capex (Rajasthan glassware plant, +55% fixed assets in FY24 alone). The earnings trajectory (PAT growth only 2.4% in FY25, PAT declining 17% in Q3 FY26) raises serious questions about whether the business can return to its peak capital efficiency. Verdict: CAUTION — post-IPO ROCE collapse, declining earnings momentum

Filter 1.3 — Debt / Leverage Net D/E was 0.3x as of June 2023 (IPO). Post-IPO capex increased long-term debt 220% by FY24 (₹277 Mn), though current ratio improved to 9.3x in FY25 and interest coverage remains high (339x in FY25). Structurally debt-light. Verdict: PASS — low absolute debt

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer No significant serial acquisitions identified. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change Consumer houseware (plastic storage, glassware, casseroles) is a relatively slow-changing industry. The writing instruments segment faces some substitution risk from digital, but is evolving slowly. The furniture segment is stable. Verdict: PASS — slow-changing core business

Filter 1.6 — Ownership / Alignment Rathod family remains largest shareholder, though diluted from ~76% pre-IPO. Promoter-led enterprise. Verdict: PASS

Gate 1 Summary: Gate 1 issues are not disqualifying in isolation, but the conglomerate structure and promoter sell-down at ROCE peak are meaningful concerns warranting heightened scrutiny.

COMPANY 2 — HAWKINS COOKERS

CMP: ₹7,815.00 | P/E: 31.51x | Market Cap: ₹4,134 Cr | ROCE: 40.89%

Business Description

Hawkins Cookers (est. 1959) is India's leading pressure cooker brand with 65+ years of market presence. GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

No SEBI actions, regulatory censures, or related-party controversies have been identified. ICRA, in its latest rating review, notes compliance with all obligations. The sole minor compliance breach cited in the 2023-24 annual report was a 9-hour delay in submitting AGM proceedings — a trivial administrative matter. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check Not a turnaround story. Hawkins has been consistently profitable for 60+ years. No structural business reinvention or turnaround dependency. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.3 — Debt / Leverage Net debt is firmly negative: net cash of ₹356 Cr as of March 2025, ₹131 Cr as of Sep 2025. Long-term debt is minimal (₹163 Mn in FY25 — primarily against working capital facilities). D/E ratio is effectively zero. Interest coverage is comfortably >100x. Cash materially exceeds debt. Verdict: PASS — Ideal balance sheet, debt-free

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer Zero acquisitions in the last 10+ years. All growth is organic. No goodwill on the balance sheet. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change Pressure cookers and cookware are among the most stable product categories in existence. The product form has not meaningfully changed in 60 years. Technology disruption risk is negligible — gas/induction cooktops evolve but the pressure cooker adapts incrementally. This is exactly the kind of "slow-changing industry" the Darwin framework prizes. Verdict: PASS — Quintessentially stable industry

Filter 1.6 — Ownership / Alignment Vasudeva family (promoter group) holds 56.03% — a concentrated, stable, entrepreneurial ownership structure. Promoter holding has remained unchanged for multiple consecutive quarters. Verdict: PASS

KEY DARWIN INSIGHTS FROM SECTOR 80

1. Convergence delivers — and concentrates — in this sector. Of 9 companies, only 1 (Hawkins, 11%) clears all Darwin gates on quality. The houseware sector is structurally challenging: Chinese price competition, unorganised sector dominance, and commodity raw materials combine to destroy ROCE for most participants.

2. Brand durability > product complexity. Hawkins outperforms because it has focused relentlessly on one product category (pressure cookers/cookware) with three brand tiers for 65 years. Cello tried to be "everything" and ROCE has collapsed. Focused moats compound; diversified spread destroys capital.

3. The IPO trap. Cello World's IPO was a 100% OFS where promoters crystallised returns at peak ROCE (44%). Since listing, ROCE has collapsed to 17%. This is a textbook example of why Darwin investors avoid IPO euphoria — the promoter selling is the best possible signal that the high ROCE era was ending.

4. Patient capital works. Hawkins Cookers is the answer to "what does a Darwin-quality business in India look like?" It is priced accordingly — at 31.51x, it will likely never be cheap. Entry requires a market-wide crisis or a sector-specific demand shock.

5. Size matters for Darwin investing. Five of the nine companies in this sector (Maruti Interior, Gothi Plascon, Riddhi Display, Inflame Appliances, Gorani Industries) have market caps below ₹400 Cr. Darwin-style permanent ownership requires businesses with durable, scalable moats. Micro-caps rarely qualify.
