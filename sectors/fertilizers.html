PART 1 — INDUSTRY OVERVIEW & LATEST UPDATES (May 2026)

1.1 Market Size & Growth

India's fertilizer market generated revenue of USD 19,363.7 million in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 25,790.1 million by 2033, growing at a CAGR of 3.6% (Grand View Research, Jan 2026). A broader Renub Research estimate puts the market at USD 44.62 Billion in 2025, growing to USD 75.96 Billion by 2034 at a CAGR of 6.09%, depending on scope of coverage (including imports and subsidy pass-through).

India accounts for 8.5% of the global fertilizer market in 2025.

Overall domestic fertilizer production rose 2.7% to 51.9 million tonnes in FY 2024–25, though urea output dipped 2.4% to 30.6 MT as part of a deliberate policy shift toward balanced nutrient use.

1.2 Policy Environment — Critical to Every Company in This Sector

⚠️ DARWIN FRAMEWORK NOTE: The fertilizer sector is one of the most heavily regulated, government-subsidised industries in India. Every company's revenue, margins, and working capital are directly determined by government subsidy policy. This is a structural feature that profoundly affects the Darwin framework analysis for every company below.

Key Policy Facts (FY 2025–26 / FY 2026–27):

  • Total Fertilizer Subsidy Budget FY 2025–26: ~₹1.68 lakh crore (BE); revised to ~₹1.86 lakh crore
  • Urea Subsidy: ~₹1.19 lakh crore. Urea MRP remains fixed at ₹242 per 45-kg bag since March 2018 — completely controlled
  • P&K Subsidy (NBS Scheme): ₹49,000 crore. NBS rates are revised bi-annually (Kharif + Rabi)
  • Kharif 2026 NBS Budget Approved (Apr 2026): ₹41,533.81 crore — ₹4,317 crore higher than Kharif 2025
  • Budget 2026–27 Signal: Subsidy outlay projected at ₹1.70 lakh crore, down from ₹1.86 lakh crore (RE 2025–26). The Economic Survey 2025–26 advocates for potential urea price adjustments + direct income transfers — a structural reform signal
  • DAP Special Package: Over and above NBS, government provides ₹3,500/MT special DAP subsidy (extended through Kharif 2026) to prevent MRP hike at ₹27,000/MT
  • NBS rates for phosphorus raised: ₹43,600 → ₹47,960/tonne (Rabi FY26); sulphur ₹2,610 → ₹2,870/tonne

1.3 Raw Material & Supply Chain Risks (CRITICAL — May 2026)

This is the biggest live risk to the sector right now:

  • DAP Import Crisis: India imported only 49,000 tonnes of DAP in April 2026 — total Jan–Apr 2026 imports were just 298,000 tonnes, only 39% of the same period in 2025
  • Hormuz Strait Disruption: The Iran–Israel–US geopolitical conflict has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, severely disrupting LNG, ammonia, and fertilizer shipping routes (March–May 2026)
  • Global DAP Price: Hovering near $760–$781/MT CFR — more than 50% above the prior price level of $515–$525. Jordan contract in mid-2025 was at $781.50/MT
  • Rupee Depreciation: INR approaching ₹93/USD — a ₹2 fall adds over ₹1,200/tonne to DAP import costs, squeezing margins and inflating subsidy burden
  • India imports 100% of its DAP requirements — domestically produced DAP is made from imported phosphate rock and sulfur
  • OCP Morocco dominates: Supplies ~20% of India's phosphate rock and 1/3rd of phosphoric acid; its products cover over 90% of India's DAP consumption
  • FY 2025–26 Imports Surged 50% YoY: In first 10 months (Apr–Jan), India's fertilizer imports rose to ~20.9 million tonnes — compounding subsidy and forex pressure

1.4 Structural Industry Characteristics (Darwin "Outside View")

From a Darwin/Nalanda "convergence" perspective, the Indian fertilizer industry has several structural features that make it inherently difficult to generate consistently high ROCE:

  1. Price controls: Urea MRP frozen since 2018. P&K MRPs monitored by government. Companies cannot price freely.
  2. Subsidy receivables risk: Companies are often owed large sums by the government that arrive with delays — affecting working capital and FCF
  3. Raw material imports: Phosphate rock, potash, sulfur, ammonia — nearly all imported. No domestic pricing power
  4. Commodity cyclicality: Input prices (natural gas, phosphate, ammonia) are globally volatile
  5. Government ownership: Multiple large players (FACT, RCF, NFL, GSFC partially) are PSUs — automatically disqualified under Darwin framework
  6. Capital intensive: Fertilizer plants require heavy ongoing capex; FCF generation is structurally challenged
  7. Subsidy reform risk: Budget 2026–27 signals gradual subsidy rationalisation — a potential long-term headwind for companies reliant on subsidy-supported volumes

Industry Verdict (Outside View): The fertilizer industry in India does NOT naturally allow companies to earn consistently high returns above cost of capital. Most companies in this screener have ROCE well below 20%. Only 3–4 companies have demonstrated sustained ROCE above the Nalanda threshold.

PART 3 — SECTOR-LEVEL DARWIN VERDICT

Why Most Fertilizer Companies Fail the Darwin Framework

The Darwin/Nalanda framework is extremely demanding, and the Indian fertilizer sector structurally produces companies that fail for these systematic reasons:

  1. PSU Dominance: 5 of the top 9 companies by market cap are government-owned (FACT, RCF, GSFC, NFL, Madras Fertilizers). All are automatically disqualified under the framework.

  2. Price Controls: Urea MRP has been frozen at ₹242/bag since 2018. No pricing power = structurally capped ROCE for urea manufacturers.

  3. Subsidy Receivable Risk: Government delays in subsidy payments create working capital stress and distort FCF.

  4. Import Dependency: India imports ~100% of its potash and >50% of DAP requirements. With DAP imports down 61% in Jan–Apr 2026 and prices at $760+/MT, the sector faces acute input cost pressure.

  5. Capital Intensity: Fertilizer manufacturing requires heavy ongoing capex — structurally negative or lumpy FCF is common.

  6. ROCE Ceiling: In a price-controlled, subsidy-dependent sector, achieving sustained ROCE >20% is the exception, not the rule. Only Chambal (25.5%) and Coromandel (22.7%) consistently achieve this.

The Two Private-Sector Winners Under This Framework

Only Coromandel International and Chambal Fertilisers clear all Darwin gates. Chambal additionally clears the valuation gate.
