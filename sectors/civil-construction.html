SECTION 1: INDUSTRY OUTSIDE VIEW (The Convergence Test)

Darwin Principle: Before picking individual stocks, assess whether the industry itself allows participants to earn returns above the cost of capital sustainably. If the median industry ROCE < 12–15%, only outliers survive — and their advantages must be structurally durable.

1.1 Industry Macro Backdrop (Latest Data: 2025–2026)

India's civil construction sector is one of the most actively government-backed industries in the world right now.

Scale and Growth: - The Indian construction market reached ₹5.31 lakh crore (~USD 300 billion) in 2025, growing 11.2% YoY. - The sector is forecast to cross ₹39.10 lakh crore by 2029, at a CAGR of ~8.8%. - Construction output grew 8.1% in real terms in 2025, with 6.4% projected for 2026. - The IIP index for infrastructure and construction goods rose ~12.1% YoY in November 2025, signalling accelerating demand.

Government Capex Firepower: - Union Budget FY2025–26: ₹11.21 lakh crore allocated to infrastructure (3.1% of GDP — highest ever). - NHAI FY2025–26: Constructed 5,313 km of highways — 15% above target (build pace: 14.5 km/day). Capex reached ₹2.44 trillion. - September 2025: Government announced ₹11 lakh crore initiative to build 17,000 km of expressways by 2033 (fivefold expansion). - NHAI FY2026 pipeline: Already invited bids for 2,188 km at ₹1.15 lakh crore; total pipeline exceeds ₹3 lakh crore. - Railways FY2026 Budget: ₹2.65 lakh crore — funding station redevelopment, DFC Phase 2, metro. - NIP (National Infrastructure Pipeline): Expanded from original ₹1.11 lakh crore to ₹2.08–2.18 lakh crore by late 2025, with transport and energy comprising ~55%.

1.2 Industry ROCE Profile — The "Outside View"

Darwin's Convergence Test: What does the industry's return on capital look like at the median? This is the base rate before picking winners.

Analysing the Top 50 companies from the screener data:

Companies with ROCE >30% 6 out of 50 (12%)
Companies with negative ROCE 2 (BGR Energy, Simplex Infra)
Industry median FCF (5-yr) Mixed — many are FCF-negative

Darwin Verdict on Industry Attractiveness: ⚠️ MODERATE

The industry median ROCE of ~16–17% clears the minimum 12–15% bar. However, this is a government-dependent, working capital-intensive, asset-heavy industry. Most companies require significant debt or working capital lines to fund project execution. True economic returns after accounting for the full working capital cycle and execution risk are materially lower than reported ROCE for many players.

Structural Challenges (Key Risks): - Delayed government payments continue to strain contractors' cash flows - Working capital cycles have lengthened — bank credit to the sector grew ~13% in FY2025 just to fund working capital - ICRA expects the interest coverage ratio for the industry to remain at a modest 3.6–3.9x in FY2026 - Competitive bidding intensity remains high; margins for road EPC companies are thin at 11–13% EBITDA on average - Land acquisition disputes remain the single largest cause of project delays - Monsoon seasonality creates Q2 revenue troughs every year - Stricter banking regulations (higher provisions for under-construction infra projects) deferred to March 2026 — introduces uncertainty

Structural Tailwinds: - Multi-year government order visibility (NIP, Bharatmala, DFC all span 2026–2033) - NHAI payment cycles improving from 90 to ~60 days average - InvIT recycling reduces the need for pure debt leverage at the HAM/BOT operator level - Government tightening bidding norms — filtering out undercapitalised, aggressive bidders

1.3 Industry Classification for Darwin Framework

Darwin Filter Industry Assessment
Pace of change (disruption risk) LOW — Roads and rail construction is 100+ year old technology
Technological obsolescence risk LOW — No tech disruption possible for physical infrastructure
Government dependency HIGH — 63% of outlays are public; policy risk exists
Working capital intensity HIGH — Structurally cash-consumptive business model
Margin variability MODERATE-HIGH — Input costs (steel, cement) create cyclicality
Competitive intensity HIGH — Fragmented players; aggressive bidding
Moat availability NARROW — Scale, execution track record, pre-qualification matter

Key Darwin Insight on This Industry: Most EPC construction companies are structurally difficult for permanent Nalanda-style ownership because: 1. FCF is persistently negative due to project-based working capital cycles 2. Debt is often unavoidable (D/E ratios of 0.5–1.5x are common) 3. ROCE is lumpy, project-driven, and not easily compounding 4. Customer concentration is extreme (NHAI, MoRTH, state governments = >70% revenue for most players)

The Darwin investor must seek outliers — companies that have somehow achieved high ROCE, clean balance sheets, and durable competitive positions within this structurally difficult industry. Such outliers exist but are rare.

SECTION 2: COMPANY-BY-COMPANY GATE ANALYSIS

Methodology

Each company is first screened using Gate 1 elimination filters. Only companies clearing all 6 Gate 1 filters proceed to Gate 2 quality and valuation analysis.

Gate 1 Hard Disqualifiers (any one = immediate elimination): - Promoter integrity issues / pledging > 20% / regulatory action - Turnaround story (losses or ROCE < cost of capital for 3+ years) - Debt/Equity > 0.3 OR interest coverage < 10x - Serial acquirer (>3 acquisitions in 10 years) - High disruption risk industry (N/A here — civil construction is stable) - Promoter < 25% shareholding
