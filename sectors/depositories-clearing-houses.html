"Selecting for ONE great trait brings along many other good traits for free — just as selecting for tameness in Siberian foxes produced floppy ears, piebald fur, and docility." Selecting for high ROCE in this industry brings: regulatory moat, network effects, zero debt, capital-light models, and permanent revenue streams.

Analysis Date: May 2026 | Data Source: Screener, FY26 Quarterly Results, Axis Capital Research, AMFI

🏭 INDUSTRY OVERVIEW — THE OUTSIDE VIEW FIRST

What This Industry Does

India's capital market infrastructure is a four-layer ecosystem — each layer a natural oligopoly or duopoly:

Layer Players Function
Depositories CDSL, NSDL Hold securities in demat form; manage 18+ crore accounts
Registrar & Transfer Agents (RTAs) CAMS (~68% share), KFin (~32% share) Process MF transactions; maintain investor records
Clearing Corporations NSE Clearing, BSE Clearing Settle stock exchange trades
Other Intermediaries Beacon Trusteeship Niche SEBI-regulated roles (debenture trustees)

Why This Industry Is Structurally Exceptional

  1. Regulated Oligopoly / Duopoly: SEBI creates formidable barriers. Only CDSL and NSDL are licensed depositories. Only CAMS and KFin are the dominant RTAs. New entrants face a multi-year SEBI licensing process, mandatory capital requirements, and zero switching by AMCs/DPs.

  2. Volume × Licence Business Model: Revenue is a function of transaction volumes, account counts, and AUM — all of which grow with India's financialisation. Incremental cost of serving a new account or processing a new SIP is near-zero (extreme operating leverage).

  3. Mission-Critical, Never-Discretionary: These companies are the plumbing of Indian capital markets. Banks don't cancel their CDSL/NSDL relationship. AMCs cannot switch RTAs casually — switching costs are enormous (data migration, IT re-integration, investor communication).

  4. Secular Tailwind — India's Financialisation Decade: - Demat accounts: 5.5 crore (Mar 2021) → 18+ crore (Mar 2026) → structural runway as penetration is <15% - MF AUM: ₹32 lakh crore (Apr 2021) → ₹81.92 lakh crore (Apr 2026) — 3x in 5 years - Bain projects individual MF AUM to cross ₹300 lakh crore in a decade - Industry AUM projected to surpass ₹300 trillion by 2035 (ICRA Analytics) - Only ~10% of India's population participates in equity markets today

  5. Industry ROCE Profile: Median ROCE across the 4 quality players exceeds 30% — well above Nalanda's 20% threshold. This is the convergence (outside view) test result. Industry passes decisively.

Key Risks to Monitor

  • Regulatory Pricing Risk: SEBI periodically revises transaction fee caps (impacted CDSL's KYC revenue, CAMS's MF yield). This is the primary swing factor in short-term earnings.
  • Market Cyclicality: New account openings and trading volumes are correlated with market sentiment. Demat additions slowed sharply in early 2025 during market correction.
  • Technology: Not a disruption risk (these companies ARE the tech layer) but requires continuous capex in system resilience and new features.
  • NSDL IPO: Expected to intensify investor attention on CDSL comparables and competitive dynamics.

🔬 INDIVIDUAL COMPANY ANALYSIS — DARWIN GATES

1️⃣ CDSL (Central Depository Services India Ltd)

CMP: ₹1,204 | TTM PE: 55.2x | Market Cap: ₹25,166 Cr

Background

Asia's only listed depository. Backed historically by BSE (major shareholder). SEBI-regulated. Serves 18+ crore demat accounts as of March 2026, having grown from 15.3 crore in March 2025. The NSDL IPO (competitor) is expected shortly, adding a second listed player to the depository segment.

✅ GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter/Management Integrity CDSL is a SEBI-regulated infrastructure entity — governance is overseen by the regulator itself. BSE was the major promoter, but SEBI mandated reduction of exchange stakes in depositories to below 24%. No history of fraud, related-party abuse, or regulatory censure by management. MD & CEO Nehal Vora has maintained a clean public record. In May 2026, CDSL board shortlisted names for Executive Director positions in two new verticals, awaiting SEBI approval — showing structured corporate governance. No promoter pledge concerns (institutional/regulated ownership). → PASS

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check CDSL is not a turnaround. It is a profitable, structurally growing business. ROCE has consistently exceeded 25%+ over 5 years. This is not a business being analysed on hope — it has delivered actual earnings and FCF. → PASS

Filter 1.3 — Debt/Leverage CDSL is effectively debt-free. Screener shows "Company has reduced debt." FCF has been consistently positive over 5 years (₹1,348 Cr cumulative) and 3 years (₹1,046 Cr). The capital-light model means no debt is required to grow. → PASS

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer CDSL is not a serial acquirer. Its subsidiaries include CDSL Ventures (KYC services) and CDSL Insurance (insurance repository) — both organic extensions of the core depository business, not external acquisitions. No goodwill inflation. → PASS

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change Depositories are the opposite of fast-changing. NSDL and CDSL have existed since the late 1990s. No new entrant has materialized in 25 years. The regulatory licence is the moat — not the technology. The industry serves equity markets, which will exist for decades. The primary risk is regulatory fee changes, not disruption. → PASS

Filter 1.6 — Ownership Alignment Here is CDSL's key governance nuance: there is no single founder-promoter with dominant economic ownership. BSE has been reducing its stake (SEBI mandated below 24%). Major shareholders are a mix of BSE, institutional investors, and public. Effective management is professional (MD & CEO Nehal Vora). This is a weak point relative to Nalanda's ideal — they prefer founder-led companies. However, CDSL's regulatory oversight by SEBI acts as a substitute for promoter alignment. Risk of principal-agent problem exists but is structurally mitigated. → CONDITIONAL PASS (No founder; regulatory governance substitutes partially. Note this as a structural caveat.)

📝 INDUSTRY THESIS — ONE PARAGRAPH

India's capital market infrastructure companies are the toll booths on the highway of India's financialisation. Every demat account, every SIP, every corporate bond, every stock trade must flow through CDSL/NSDL (for custody) and CAMS/KFin (for MF records). India's equity market penetration is below 15% of the population, MF AUM is projected to grow 4x in a decade, and SIP culture is permanently embedding itself in Indian household behaviour. These businesses earn higher ROCE (30–50%) than almost any other regulated financial entity in India, require near-zero capital to grow, and face no credible threat of new entrants or technology disruption — they are the technology layer. The only question is price. At current multiples, patience is required. The businesses are extraordinary; the prices are not yet.
