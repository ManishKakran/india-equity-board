PART I — INDUSTRY ANALYSIS (Outside View / Convergence Test)

Industry Overview: Indian Consulting Services Sector

The Indian consulting services sector spans a heterogeneous mix of sub-verticals — infrastructure consulting (roads, highways, railways, water), IT/cyber security consulting, carbon credit advisory, management consulting, survey & mapping, and financial/management consultancy. The 29 companies in this list range from market caps of ₹3.82 Cr to ₹836 Cr, making this almost entirely a micro-cap and small-cap universe.

Structural Tailwinds (2025–2031):

The India management consulting services market is expanding sharply. It was valued at USD 8.31 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 17.01 billion by 2031 at a CAGR of ~12.7%, driven by three structural forces: 1. Digital transformation spend: Corporate India's digital-initiative budgets reached USD 110 billion in FY2025, up 15% year-on-year. 72% of Indian CEOs plan double-digit annual technology budget increases through 2027 (KPMG CEO Outlook 2025), keeping consultants on retainer for change-management and process-reengineering mandates. 2. Mandatory ESG disclosures: SEBI's BRSR rules are widening the advisory addressable base from the top 150 companies to the full cohort of 1,000 listed firms by FY2026–27, creating durable demand for ESG and compliance advisory. 3. Infrastructure super-cycle: India's infrastructure pipeline (roads, highways, railways, water, metros) under PM Gati Shakti and allied programs is creating sustained multi-year demand for infrastructure project management and supervision consultancies — the largest segment among our 29 companies.

Industry ROCE Assessment (Outside View):

From the data provided, the median ROCE of this peer group sits at approximately 11–14% — barely above the cost of capital for most firms. Only a handful (IMEC Services at 177%, Marc Technocrats at 40.86%, Monarch Surveyors at 28.52%, Techknowgreen at 29.50%, and Onesource Industries at 42.03%) demonstrate ROCEs that suggest genuine competitive advantages. The majority of these companies are: - Tiny (sub-₹100 Cr market cap), with thin management depth - Dependent on government contract cycles (B2G) - Suffering from high debtor days and negative free cash flows - Lacking meaningful moats beyond incumbent relationships

Darwinian Verdict on Industry: The consulting services industry, at this small-cap level, is structurally fragmented and competitive with no dominant winner and many low-ROCE players. This is a hunting ground for rare gems, not an industry where one can invest broadly. The framework demands extreme selectivity. Only companies with structural advantages (licensing, technical certifications, domain monopolies, government framework contracts) deserve further analysis.
