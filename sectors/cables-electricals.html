PART I — INDUSTRY OVERVIEW & OUTSIDE VIEW (Convergence Test)

"We always begin with the outside view — what does this industry structurally allow its participants to earn?"

Market Size & Growth Tailwinds

India's cables and wires market is one of the most structurally advantaged industries in the country right now. The market stood at approximately ₹1.7 lakh crore (~USD 20–21 billion) in FY25 and is projected to grow at 8–15% CAGR through 2031–35, depending on the segment. India is now the third-largest wire and cable market globally, after China and the USA.

Key demand drivers — all multi-year, structural:

  1. Government infrastructure spending — ₹9.12 lakh crore allocated in FY26 Union Budget (25% YoY increase) for power, railways, and urban infrastructure. The National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP) projects ₹111 lakh crore investment over FY20–25, with 0.5–2% earmarked for cables.
  2. Renewable energy — India targets 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030. Per industry norms, a 1 MW solar project alone consumes ~50 km of cable. Solar cables, wind cables, and grid integration cables are the fastest-growing sub-segments.
  3. Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS) — ₹3.03 lakh crore outlay for DISCOM modernisation through FY26, directly boosting cable demand.
  4. Real estate upcycle — Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana, Smart Cities Mission, and Housing for All create sustained low-voltage wire demand.
  5. 5G and data centres — Only 35% of India's 4.69 lakh 5G towers are fiberised (as of early 2025), leaving massive headroom. Data-centre operators are budgeting ~USD 2 billion for fibre in this decade.
  6. EV charging infrastructure — A 30% EV adoption target by 2030 requires new categories of specialised cables.

Industry Structure Assessment (Outside View Score)

Industry organised share ~74% (growing; unorganised shrinking)

Industry concentration Low — No player >18% market share (Polycab at ~18%, KEI at ~9%, RR Kabel ~7%)
Long-term demand visibility VERY HIGH — decade-long structural tailwinds

Outside View Verdict: ATTRACTIVE industry for long-term investing, but not without competition risk. Structurally allows returns above cost of capital for well-run, scaled players. Smaller, undifferentiated players face margin compression risk from new entrants.

🔴 GATE 1 ELIMINATIONS — IMMEDIATE DISQUALIFICATIONS

The following companies are disqualified at Gate 1 before any quality analysis. Fail any one filter → stop.

❌ 2. KEI Industries (CMP: ₹5,088 | P/E: 52.97 | ROCE: 20.07%)

Filter 1.1 — PROMOTER / MANAGEMENT INTEGRITY: CONDITIONAL FAIL (WATCH)

On May 7, 2026, the Income Tax Department initiated search and seizure operations at KEI's registered office, manufacturing plants (Bhiwadi, Chopanki, Silvassa), and the residences of promoters and executives. Operations concluded May 12, 2026. The company filed disclosures under SEBI Regulation 30, confirmed cooperation, but stated: "the financial impact of these operations is currently unquantifiable." No formal IT Department order has been received as of this report date.

This is a critical Gate 1.1 concern. Under the Darwin framework, integrity of the promoter is non-negotiable. An IT search extending to promoter residences is a more serious signal than a routine corporate audit. While IT raids do not automatically imply wrongdoing, the framework demands we avoid companies where even a shadow of integrity risk exists — because the cost of being wrong is permanent capital loss.

Filter 1.2 (Turnaround): PASS — Not a turnaround story.

Filter 1.3 (Debt): BORDERLINE — FCF has been deeply negative over 5 years (-₹342 crore over 5 years, -₹928 crore over 3 years, -₹726 crore in the prior annual period). This indicates the company funds its growth heavily through working capital and external financing rather than internal cash generation. Nearly debt-free on a net basis, but persistent negative FCF is concerning for a company at its scale.

GATE 1 VERDICT: ❌ DISQUALIFY — Promoter residence IT search (May 2026) triggers Filter 1.1 watch; combined with persistently negative FCF (Filter 1.3 concern), the company cannot clear Gate 1 at this time. Re-evaluate only after IT Department issues formal orders and outcome is clear. If no material wrongdoing is found, restore to watchlist and re-run Gate 2.

❌ 4. Finolex Cables (CMP: ₹1,022 | P/E: 22.96 | ROCE: 17.67%)

Key facts: - Deepak Chhabria's re-election as Executive Chairman was defeated at the AGM but he continued to run operations anyway — a governance red flag described as "unlawful" by shareholder advisory services including Stakeholder Empowerment Services. - Secretarial auditors highlighted non-compliance in the 2020 Annual Report. - The dispute reached the Supreme Court of India in March 2025 when Orbit moved to challenge non-disclosure of AGM voting results. - Related NCLT/NCLAT proceedings have been ongoing since 2016–2019.

This is a textbook Filter 1.1 failure — a promoter-level governance and control dispute has been raging for nearly a decade, with NCLT/NCLAT/Supreme Court involvement, and minority shareholders have suffered accordingly.

GATE 1 VERDICT: ❌ DISQUALIFY — Promoter integrity and governance dispute (Filter 1.1) — active Supreme Court litigation, NCLAT proceedings, and management acting against defeated AGM resolutions. Do NOT invest regardless of financial quality.

Company 1: POLYCAB INDIA ⭐ FLAGSHIP ANALYSIS

Market Cap: ₹1,37,763 Cr | CMP: ₹9,151 | P/E: 51.56 | ROCE: 34.32%

Analysis 2.1 — Historical ROCE

Polycab is India's largest wires and cables manufacturer with ~18% market share. Its 5-year average ROCE of 34.32% is the strongest in the peer group and approaches Nalanda Capital's own portfolio median of ~42%.

ROCE Verdict: STRONG (>30%)

Analysis 2.2 — Robustness

a) Balance Sheet: FCF over 5 years is +₹4,591 crore — the only company in the universe with consistently large positive FCF. Net cash position confirmed (net debt of -₹971 crore as of Sep 2025 — i.e., cash exceeds debt). Promoter shareholding at 61.5–62.3% with zero pledge confirmed by SEBI takeover disclosure filed April 2026.

b) Competitive Advantage: Deepest distribution network with 4,000+ distributors. Brand recall in retail housing wire segment is closest to FMEG-level. Revenue grew from ₹14,100 Cr (FY23) to approximately ₹23,000+ Cr (FY26E). Q3 FY26 revenue grew 46% YoY. Also diversifying into FMEG (fans, switches, LEDs), though this is capital-light relative to cables.

c) Red Flag — December 2023 IT Search: The Income Tax Department conducted search operations at Polycab premises from December 22–30, 2023. Reports suggested ₹250–300 crore of transactions in promoter accounts. The company denied tax evasion, fully cooperated, and stated it had not received any formal communication from the IT Department on the outcome. As of May 2026, no adverse order has been made public, suggesting the matter did not result in a significant formal finding. This is a residual concern but not a disqualifying event at this time given no formal adverse determination.

d) Customer Concentration: Wires and cables revenue is spread across thousands of distributors and institutional buyers. No single customer exceeds 10–15% of revenue.

e) Industry: New entrant risk (UltraTech from Dec 2026) is a legitimate concern but manageable for Polycab given brand, distribution depth, and scale.

Robustness Verdict: HIGH (tempered by the historical IT search overhang)

Analysis 2.3 — Management Quality

Management has maintained dividend discipline, invested in FMEG diversification (expanding TAM without destroying ROCE), deployed capex efficiently, and grown revenue at a compound ~20%+ over five years. The FMEG business, while currently lower-margin, is a long-term optionality. Promoters (Jaisinghani family) are owner-operators with 61.5% stake and zero pledging.

Management Verdict: GOOD to EXCELLENT

PART VII — INDUSTRY RISK MONITOR

🚨 Top 3 Risks to Watch

1. UltraTech Cement Entry (December 2026 target) UltraTech announced ₹1,800 crore investment in a Bharuch, Gujarat plant targeting the W&C segment. This is the most significant competitive risk in the near term. Companies most exposed: Polycab and KEI (retail wire segment). Companies least exposed: industrial cable specialists (EHV segment where brand and certification matter more).

2. Copper Price Volatility Copper and aluminium constitute 40–60% of cable costs. A 23% YoY rise in copper/aluminium in Q3 FY25 compressed margins industry-wide. This is a structural, recurring risk for all players.

3. KEI Industries IT Raid Outcome The formal IT Department order (post May 2026 search) will determine whether KEI should be restored to consideration or permanently avoided. If no material wrongdoing is found, KEI becomes a watchlist candidate subject to FCF improvement.
