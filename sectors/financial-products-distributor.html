INDUSTRY OVERVIEW — Financial Products Distribution in India

Structural Tailwinds (Latest Data, May 2026)

India's mutual fund distribution industry is at an inflection point, backed by powerful and durable structural tailwinds:

  • Industry AUM: India's mutual fund industry AUM stood at ₹81.92 lakh crore (₹81.92 trillion) as of April 30, 2026, having grown 12.2% in FY26 to ₹73.73 lakh crore by March 31, 2026. AMFI projects this could double to ₹120 lakh crore by 2030.
  • SIP Momentum: Monthly SIP contributions hit a record ₹32,087 crore in March 2026, with SIP AUM at ₹15.10 lakh crore (20.5% of total MF industry AUM). SIP accounts recovered to 9.72 crore by March 2026. For 61 consecutive months since March 2021, equity MF net inflows have remained positive — a remarkable streak of retail resilience even through two major FPI exodus events.
  • Demat Accounts: Demat accounts grew to 207.1 million by H1 FY26 from 192.4 million in March 2025, a 23% CAGR over 10 years, signalling surging capital market participation.
  • Financialisation of Savings: Individual AUM from retail and HNI investors climbed from 52% of total MF AUM in March 2020 to 60.9% by September 2025, showing a structural shift away from physical assets (gold, real estate) toward financial instruments.
  • B30 City Penetration: Bottom-30 city locations have been growing at twice the rate of Top-30 cities. SEBI incentivises expansion into tier-2/3 markets with special commission structures — ₹2,000 per new investor from B30 cities.

Regulatory Landscape — SEBI 2026 Overhaul

  • New MF Regulations (effective April 1, 2026): SEBI approved the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 on December 17, 2025, replacing the 1996 regime. Key changes include introduction of a Base Expense Ratio (BER) separating AMC management fees from other costs, lower brokerage caps (cash market: 12 bps → 6 bps; derivatives: 5 bps → 2 bps), optional performance-linked fees, and strengthened governance.
  • TER Impact on Distributors: SEBI's lower TER framework modestly compresses trail commission yields by an estimated 2–10 bps across the industry. Larger, more efficient platforms (Anand Rathi Wealth, Prudent Corp) are better positioned to absorb this than smaller players. Upfront commissions have been fully phased out; trail commissions based on AUM are the dominant income model.
  • Distributor Commission Structure: In 2026, distributors earn primarily via trail commissions (0.3–1.0% per annum on regular plan AUM), which compound as AUM grows. This creates a powerful, recurring income stream aligned with long-term client retention.

Industry Convergence Test (Darwin Framework)

"If most companies in the industry can't hack it, why should I believe that this company is unique?"

Outside View Assessment: Does the financial products distribution industry structurally allow consistent returns above cost of capital?

Answer: YES — with a critical caveat on scale. The top 2–3 non-bank distributors (Anand Rathi Wealth, Prudent Corp, NJ India Invest) consistently earn ROCE of 30–57%, with high asset-light models, trail-commission-based recurring revenues, and zero inventory risk. The economics are highly attractive at scale — low capex, high operating leverage, and AUM that compounds even without proportional cost increases. However, the industry is bifurcated: the top platforms thrive while sub-scale players (as seen in Dharni Capital, Sodhani Capital, Vedant Asset) struggle to generate meaningful returns. The convergence conclusion is: Industry is attractive ONLY for dominant, scaled platforms. Sub-scale participants face a structural ROCE disadvantage.
