PART I — INDUSTRY OUTSIDE VIEW (Convergence Test)

Before screening individual stocks, Nalanda first asks: "Is this an industry worth fishing in at all?"

1.1 Industry Snapshot (2025–2026)

India's chemicals industry is at a structural inflection point. The broad sector is valued at ~US$220 billion and was growing at a CAGR of ~9% toward a projected US$300 billion by FY2025. McKinsey projects it to reach US$255 billion by 2030, with India potentially tripling its global chemicals market share by 2040. The Indian specialty chemicals sub-segment was valued at US$62–65 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach US$92–95 billion by 2030–2033 at a CAGR of 4–9%.

Structural Tailwinds: - China+1 supply chain diversification — global multinationals actively seeking to reduce China dependency - Domestic demand explosion: India expected to account for 20%+ of incremental global chemical consumption over two decades - Government policy support: PLI schemes, PCPIR zones, five new Centres of Excellence for chemicals approved Oct 2024 - India ranked 6th globally in chemical production, 14th in exports (excluding pharma) - BCG reports Indian chemicals delivered 28% TSR in recent years, leading emerging markets

Structural Headwinds (Critical — May 2026): - US imposed ~50% tariffs on Indian goods including chemicals in 2025. Experts say tariff headwinds will persist through 2026, denting the China+1 export story - China's oversupply (43% global market share, +10% output in 2024) is depressing global commodity chemical prices - India's chemicals trade deficit projected at ~US$31 billion in 2025 (concentrated in inorganics and polymers), indicating import dependence on raw materials - Environmental compliance costs rising steeply, disproportionately hurting SMEs - NIFTY Specialty Chemicals Index: down 4.7% over past 12 months (as of March 2026); industry PE at ~24.6x

1.2 Industry ROCE — The "Outside View" Filter

The Darwin framework demands: "What is the median ROCE of industry peers? If below 12%, the industry destroys capital."

From the 75-company universe, a rough ROCE distribution emerges:

ROCE Range Number of Companies % of Universe

Outside View Verdict: This is a highly heterogeneous industry. Nearly one-third of the universe is actively destroying capital (negative ROCE). The median ROCE of the full universe is approximately 7–9%, below the Nalanda "proceed" threshold of 12%+. However, there is a high-quality sub-cluster (Grauer & Weil, GHCL, POCL, SRF, Tanfac, Vikram Thermo, Alufluoride, Sr.Rayala.Hypo, ARCL Organics, J.G.Chemicals, Amines & Plastics) that merits deep analysis.

Conclusion: Fish selectively in the top quartile. Do not bottom-fish the distressed majority.

PART V — INDUSTRY THEMES & DARWIN INSIGHTS

5.1 What the Data Tells Us

Theme 1 — Commodity vs Specialty Split The chemicals universe splits sharply into commodity players (low/negative ROCE, cyclical FCF, high debt) and specialty/niche players (ROCE 18%+, positive FCF, low debt). The Darwin framework efficiently filters to the latter.

Theme 2 — FCF as Truth Serum Many companies show acceptable ROCE on the P&L but fail on FCF (negative over 3–5 years). This exposes capex intensity without proportional returns — exactly what ROCE alone misses. GHCL and Sr.Rayala.Hypo are rare combinations of acceptable ROCE and exceptional FCF.

Theme 3 — Valuation Bifurcation The mid-tier companies (ROCE 15–20%) are split between deep value (P/E 8–13x) and expensive (P/E 25–60x). The Darwin framework would only buy at the cheap end — GHCL (8.86x) and Sr.Rayala.Hypo (8.53x) stand out dramatically.

Theme 4 — The Tariff Wildcard (2026) US tariffs of ~50% on Indian chemicals are a near-term headwind. However, for domestically-focused companies like GHCL (soda ash, domestic glass & detergent demand) and Grauer & Weil (industrial finishing for Indian manufacturing), this is less relevant than for export-focused players.

5.2 The Darwinian Metaphor Applied

The chemicals industry demonstrates "punctuated equilibrium" — long periods of stability interrupted by shocks (COVID supply chain boom 2021-22, post-COVID correction 2023-24, US tariff shock 2025). The best time to buy Darwinian-quality businesses in this sector is during the punctuation events — exactly now, in 2026, when the sector is down 4.7% year-on-year and sentiment is poor.
