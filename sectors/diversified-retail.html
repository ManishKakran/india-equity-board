📊 Industry Overview: Indian Diversified Retail (2025–2026)

Macro Context

India's retail market reached USD 1,060 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow at 11.4% CAGR to USD 3,121 billion by 2035. The organised retail segment is targeting ₹15 lakh crore by 2027, growing at 10–12% annually. Tier II & III city expansion and rising disposable incomes are the primary structural growth drivers.

Competitive Landscape (Latest, 2025–26)

  • Reliance Retail dominates with 19,592+ stores and ₹84,171 Cr quarterly revenues (Q1 FY26), dwarfing all listed retailers.
  • Quick Commerce Disruption — Blinkit (market leader), Zepto, and Swiggy Instamart are disrupting urban grocery retail. However, CLSA estimates quick commerce will account for less than 20% of urban consumption by 2035, limiting structural damage to large-format retailers.
  • DMart (Avenue Supermarts) expanded to 500 stores by April 2026 (415 in March 2025). Revenue of ₹59,358 Cr in FY25. The company has visibility for 2,200 eventual stores but is currently guiding only 40–70 new stores annually.
  • Vishal Mega Mart is the fastest-growing large diversified retailer — 696 stores, ₹11,260 Cr revenue in FY25, near-debt-free, PE-backed IPO in late 2024, and profitable with 14% operating margins.

Industry Median ROCE (Peers, Excluding Distressed)

Based on companies with positive ROCE: ~14–17%. Only Avenue Supermarts (~17%), Patel Retail (~17%), and Jay Ambe Supermarkets (~24%) approach or exceed the Nalanda 20% threshold. This is a structurally low-ROCE industry — meaning very few companies pass Gate 2.

Key Risks to Watch

  1. Quick commerce cannibalization of grocery mix in top-20 cities
  2. Fashion e-commerce (Meesho, Myntra) pressuring apparel-focused retailers
  3. Reliance Retail's aggressive omnichannel expansion
  4. Rising real estate and rental costs in premium locations
  5. CEO transition at DMart (Anshul Asawa replaced Ignatius Noronha, effective Feb 2026)

💡 Sector-Level Darwin Conclusion

"The outside view says: Indian diversified retail is a low-ROCE industry. Median ROCE of this 19-company universe is approximately 10–12%, well below the 20% Nalanda threshold. Only 1–2 companies (Jay Ambe, and historically DMart) have demonstrated ROCE above 20% consistently. This is consistent with the convergence/outside view test — most businesses in this sector should be avoided regardless of story quality. The rare exceptions (DMart's moat, Jay Ambe's micro-operations) are worth watching but not rushing into."

The Indian retail sector rewards: - Owned real estate (DMart model) over leased - Value/EDLP positioning over premium/aspirational - Tier II/III geography over metro-only plays - Apparel + grocery diversification over pure-play electronics or department stores - Debt-free operations — working capital intensity means leverage destroys equity value quickly

For a Darwin/Nalanda-style portfolio: This sector should be underweighted. At most 1 position (DMart on deep correction) is warranted. The quality-at-fair-price combination that Nalanda requires simply doesn't exist in this sector at current prices.
