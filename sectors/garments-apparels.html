Industry Structure (India Garments & Apparels)

India's garments and apparels industry is the country's second-largest employment generator with over 45 million people employed. The domestic market is valued at ~USD 225 billion (2025) growing at a 10–12% CAGR, with an export base of ~USD 36.5 billion in FY25. Ready Made Garments (RMG) constitute 45% of textile exports. India ranks among the top 5 global garment exporters.

Key Macro Tailwinds (2025–2026): - Rising per capita income (projected from USD 1,379 in FY25 to USD 4,000 by FY30) - E-commerce penetration and D2C brand proliferation - PLI scheme and MITRA park infrastructure investments targeting USD 100 billion exports by 2030 - China+1 sourcing diversification benefiting Indian manufacturers - RoSCTL scheme extended to support garment export competitiveness

Tariff Headwind — Critical Risk (2025–2026): India's garments faced a sequence of US tariff shocks. The US initially imposed ~27% reciprocal tariffs on Indian goods (April 2025), which then spiked to 50% in August 2025 due to a surcharge tied to India's Russian oil purchases, before the US Supreme Court invalidated the IEEPA basis in February 2026, bringing the effective rate down to ~10% Section 122 + MFN base rates (~27–42% all-in on apparel). This has forced export-heavy players to: 1. Accept 5–8% price discounts from US buyers on existing orders 2. Redirect export capacity toward European and Middle Eastern markets 3. Compete more aggressively in the domestic market

Bangladesh gained relative competitiveness at 20% Section 122 vs. India's 25%, creating margin pressure on pure-play exporters like Gokaldas Exports and Pearl Global. Domestic-focused players (Page Industries, Cantabil, Kewal Kiran) are structurally insulated from this risk.

PART VI — KEY INVESTMENT RISKS FOR THIS INDUSTRY (2025–2026)

  1. US Tariff Regime Uncertainty — All-in rates of 27–42% on Indian apparel vs. Bangladesh at ~26–36% create competitive disadvantage for pure-play exporters. IEEPA invalidation in Feb 2026 brought some relief, but MFN base rates remain high.

  2. Consumer Demand Slowdown — Urban consumption growth slowed to 5–7% in FY25 vs. 12–15% pre-COVID. Page Industries is in the eighth consecutive quarter of below-double-digit revenue growth.

  3. E-commerce Disruption — Fast fashion platforms (Shein-equivalent domestic players, Myntra, Meesho) are altering price expectations in the mid-market segment. Branded premium players are relatively insulated.

  4. China+1 Opportunity — India gained 16% US import market share in 2025 (US imports from India rose 16% in Aug 2025) at China's expense — a structural tailwind for export-oriented manufacturers.

  5. Raw Material Costs — Cotton price cycles affect margins for most players. Page Industries has shown ability to protect margins through pricing power. Commodity players (Rupa, Dollar, Lux) remain exposed.

  6. Retail Expansion Risk — Brands expanding via CAPEX-heavy store rollouts (Cantabil, Indian Terrain) face operating leverage risk in a demand slowdown.
