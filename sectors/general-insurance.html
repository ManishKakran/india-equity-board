Industry Context: Why General Insurance Is Structurally Interesting

Market Size & Growth Trajectory

The Indian general insurance market is in a sustained structural growth phase. The industry grew at 13.2% in 2023, and is forecast to reach GWP of ₹4.89 trillion (US$57.3 billion) by 2028, implying a CAGR of ~9.9%. The most recent GlobalData (April 2026) projection extends this further: non-life premiums are expected to grow at a CAGR of 10% between 2026 and 2030, from an estimated ₹3.6 trillion to ₹5.4 trillion (US$62.2 billion). Swiss Re (January 2026) identifies India as the strongest-growing major insurance market globally in the mid-term, with annual premium growth forecast at 6.9% between 2026 and 2030.

Key Structural Drivers (2025–2026 Update)

  1. GST Removal on Retail Health (September 2025): This is the most significant near-term catalyst. GST on retail health indemnity policies was removed effective September 2025. Q4 2025 data showed standalone health insurance premiums up 13.2% quarter-on-quarter as a result. This permanently expands the addressable market.

  2. 100% FDI Liberalisation: The Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025 received presidential assent on December 20, 2025. Operationalised from February 5, 2026, it permits wholly foreign-owned insurers for the first time — the most significant structural reform since 2000. This is expected to bring new capital and competition.

  3. Bima Sugam Digital Marketplace (Launched September 17, 2025): IRDAI launched a one-stop digital insurance marketplace allowing comparison, purchase, and claims management — accelerating digitisation-led distribution.

  4. "Insurance for All by 2047" Vision: India's non-life insurance penetration was only 0.98% in 2023, far below Japan (1.75%), South Korea (1.46%), and China (1.26%). The gap represents a multi-decade runway.

  5. Segment Mix Shift: PA&H (Personal Accident & Health) has grown from 35.7% of GWP in 2021 to 40.9% in 2025, now the largest segment. Motor remains the second largest. Together they account for 72.6% of GWP.

Structural Risks to Monitor

  • Rising grievances and regulatory penalties on claims handling (flagged by GlobalData, 2026)
  • Combined ratios structurally above 100% across most private insurers — profitability dependent on investment float
  • Increased competition from potential new foreign entrants under 100% FDI regime
  • Accounting changes (1/n basis for long-term products from October 2024) making year-on-year comparisons temporarily distorted
  • GST revenue moderation in 2026 expected before re-acceleration from 2027

Darwin Framework Note on Insurance as an Asset Class

Insurance businesses have a unique financial structure. Unlike manufacturing or technology, general insurers: - Earn an underwriting "float" — premiums collected upfront, claims paid later — which is invested to generate returns - Have ROCE measured differently — the float itself is a liability, but well-managed investment of float is the engine of economic profit - Are inherently capital-intensive — solvency ratios, regulatory capital requirements, and reinsurance costs are structural constraints

This means ROCE figures for insurers are not directly comparable to Nalanda's portfolio of non-financial companies with ~42% median ROCE. The framework's 20% ROCE threshold must be applied here with the recognition that insurance ROCE above 20% is genuinely exceptional and earns a pass, while ROCE below 10% represents poor capital efficiency even by sector standards.

DARWIN FRAMEWORK — SECTOR-WIDE OBSERVATIONS

Why Only One Company Survives Gate 2

The general insurance sector in India has a structural profitability challenge: virtually all players run combined ratios above 100%, meaning underwriting is unprofitable. Investment float income is what generates returns. This makes ROCE inherently compressed for most insurers. Only ICICI Lombard — with its scale advantages, mature distribution, and improving combined ratio — has managed to sustain ROCE above the 20% Nalanda minimum threshold.

The other private players (Star Health, Go Digit, Niva Bupa) are either: - Too young to have built a ROCE track record above 20% - Structurally dependent on investment returns on a small float - Still in the phase of building scale to achieve underwriting profitability

The "Outside View / Convergence" Question

"Ask: where else has this industry structure produced companies with durable returns above cost of capital?"

In global insurance markets, the companies that have generated Nalanda-type returns over decades are those with: 1. Best-in-class underwriting discipline (combined ratio sustainably below 100%) 2. Massive float invested with Buffett-like skill (Berkshire Hathaway's GEICO) 3. Monopoly distribution advantages or regulatory moats

In the Indian context, the private general insurance industry is only ~25 years old (liberalisation was 2000). Most players are still in the scaling phase. The industry structure does allow above-cost-of-capital returns — but only for the scale leaders with proven underwriting discipline. ICICI Lombard is the only listed private insurer that has demonstrated this consistently.

What Would Change a WATCHLIST to BUY

For ICICI Lombard, the following would need to occur: 1. Market correction that drives ICICI Lombard's TTM PE from current 32.81x to below 15.3x (≤25% discount to Sensex PE) 2. At current earnings trajectory, this would imply a stock price correction of approximately 50% from current levels (₹1,695 → ≈₹830–850) 3. SECTOR CONCLUSION

The Indian General Insurance sector presents a classic Darwin dilemma: a structurally attractive industry in a long-term growth market, but with few businesses that currently pass the capital efficiency threshold. The sector is young, most players are still scaling, and the combined ratio challenge (underwriting losses covered by investment float) structurally suppresses ROCE for all but the dominant private player.

Of 6 companies analysed: - 2 eliminated immediately (GIC Re, New India Assurance) — government-owned enterprises - 3 eliminated at Gate 2 (Star Health, Go Digit, Niva Bupa) — ROCE below 20% threshold - 1 company (ICICI Lombard) qualifies on quality but not on valuation

The Darwin framework's discipline is clear: quality must be paired with a fair price. ICICI Lombard is the only business in this sector worthy of further attention — but only at approximately half its current price.
