SECTION 1 — INDUSTRY DEEP DIVE

1.1 India E-Commerce: Structural Picture (2026 Update)

India's e-commerce market is one of the world's fastest-growing digital commerce ecosystems. Key structural data points:

Parameter Value
Market Size (2026 est.) ~$147–185 billion (GMV)
FY2025–26 GMV (INR) ₹12+ lakh crore
E-commerce as % of retail ~5.4% (up from 2.8% four years ago)
CAGR projected (2026–2034) 18–20% per annum
Target by 2030 ~$350 billion (3rd largest globally)
Internet users (India) 1.1 billion projected (2025)
Mobile commerce share ~$115B / 78% of total
2026 YoY growth rate 12.4% (GlobalData estimate)

Key Structural Tailwinds: - 700M+ smartphone users; 85%+ of platform traffic from mobile apps - UPI digital payments deeply embedded across income tiers - Government GST rate cuts (Sep 2025) boosted discretionary spending - ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) disrupting incumbents — India Post made its first ONDC order in January 2026 - Amazon committed $35B additional India investment through 2030 - Reliance JioMart entered quick commerce in Oct 2024, Tata Neu launched unified super-app in Aug 2025 - Tier-3/4 cities are the fastest-growing segments

Key Structural Headwinds & Risks: - Intense competition from Amazon, Flipkart (Walmart), Reliance, Tata — all with deep pockets - Quick commerce ($3.65B market in 2026; CAGR 12.7%) is cannibalising traditional e-commerce in FMCG categories — 3 platforms control 90%+ of quick commerce GMV - ONDC creates structural pricing pressure on all incumbents - Digital Competition Bill (under deliberation) could impose restrictions on platform-seller dynamics - Customer acquisition costs remain structurally high — loyalty is fragile - Most Indian e-commerce listed companies have negative FCF and negative ROCE, reflecting the "invest now, profit later" model that has worked in the US but remains unproven in India at scale

Industry Outside View (Convergence Test for Gate 2): Of the 19 listed e-commerce companies in this universe, ONLY 3 have a positive ROCE above 10% (CarTrade, Macfos, Kaushalya). The industry median ROCE is deeply negative. This is structurally important: the e-commerce industry, as a whole, destroys capital. This means the Darwin framework demands extraordinary evidence that an individual company has a durable structural advantage before any investment.

1. Industry-level Capital Destruction: Of 19 companies, 16 have negative ROCE or ROCE below 10%. The sector median ROCE is deeply negative.
