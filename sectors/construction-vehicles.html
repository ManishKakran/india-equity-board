1. Industry Overview — India Construction Equipment Sector

Market Size & Growth

India's construction equipment market is one of the fastest-growing globally. The market generated approximately USD 8.55–15.37 billion in 2025 (estimates vary by scope across research firms), and is forecast to grow at a CAGR of 7.5–9.3% through 2031–2034, potentially reaching USD 13.6–29.5 billion by 2031–2034. India currently accounts for 5.6% of the global construction equipment market and is the world's third-largest market for construction equipment.

Key 2025 Segment Breakdown: - Earthmoving Equipment — Largest segment at ~56.6% share; backhoe loaders dominate volumes - Material Handling Machinery — ~45% revenue share; fastest-growing sub-segment - Road Construction Equipment — Posted 6.3% growth in FY26; projected 10.05% CAGR through 2031 - Concrete Equipment — Broadly stable; key beneficiary of infrastructure push

FY26 Reality Check — A Difficult Year

Despite the structural growth story, FY2025-26 was a challenging year for the industry:

  • Total industry sales fell ~2% YoY to 1,36,995 units (vs. 1,40,191 in FY25) per ICEMA data
  • Domestic demand declined ~7% across most equipment categories
  • Exports surged 32% YoY to record levels, partially offsetting domestic weakness

Headwinds in FY26: - Delays in land acquisition slowing highway project execution - Fewer project awards from central and state governments - Reduced pace of National Highway construction - Moderation in Jal Jeevan Mission implementation - Contractor payment delays — states with elections held back payments, severely hurting working capital - CEV Stage V emission norm transition costs in 2025 - Rising crude oil and bitumen costs impacting contractor margins

2. Company Profiles

2.1 BEML Ltd (BSE: BEML)

Sector: Defence & Aerospace, Rail & Metro, Mining & Construction Headquarters: Bengaluru | Founded: 1964 | Type: Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) under Ministry of Defence

BEML is a Schedule-A PSU, the most diversified player in this universe. It is far more than a construction equipment company — its three core divisions are Rail & Metro (38% of FY24 revenue), Mining & Construction (43%), and Defence & Aerospace (19%).

Recent Developments (2025–2026): - Won a ₹405 crore contract from BMRCL (Bengaluru Metro Rail Corporation) for standard-gauge metro cars, triggering a 22% stock rally in two trading sessions - Analysts expect Rail & Metro to pick up momentum in FY26 given Mumbai Metro 4, 5 & 6 pipeline and Bhubaneswar Metro - Signed MoU with Siemens India to jointly explore semi-high-speed and suburban passenger train opportunities - Signed MoU with STX Engine (South Korea) for co-developing Battle Tank and marine engines, targeting Indian defence needs and exports - Signed MoU with Dragflow S.R.L. (Italy) to strengthen indigenous dredging solutions - Announced a new mining equipment manufacturing plant in Chhattisgarh (dump trucks, water sprinklers, motor graders) - Q2 FY26 total income: ₹846.13 crore — up 31.7% QoQ but down 3.3% YoY; net profit declined due to execution lumpiness

Investment Characteristics: - Government ownership means policy visibility but also execution risks tied to government capex cycles - High P/E (61x) largely reflects PSU scarcity premium and large-order optionality - ROCE of 15.56% reasonable for a capital-heavy diversified manufacturer; 5-yr ROE of 7.52% is modest - FCF has improved — ₹357.73 Cr in last annual, cumulative ₹900.74 Cr over 3 years, suggesting improving cash generation - 52-week high: ₹5,488 (July 2024); 52-week low: ₹2,350 (March 2025); CMP ₹1,830 is well below peak

2.2 Action Construction Equipment Ltd — ACE (NSE: ACE)

Sector: Cranes, Construction Equipment, Agri Equipment, Material Handling Headquarters: Faridabad, Haryana | Founded: 1995 | Promoter: Vijay Agarwal family (65.4% holding)

ACE is India's largest crane manufacturer and a strong domestic brand with 8 manufacturing plants in Faridabad. Its product range includes pick-and-carry cranes (market leader), hydra cranes, mobile cranes, crawler cranes, tower cranes, backhoe loaders, motor graders, vibratory rollers, aerial work platforms, forklifts, and agricultural equipment.

Recent Developments (2025–2026): - Q3 FY26 (Oct–Dec 2025): Revenue ₹85,463 lakh (down 2% YoY), PAT ₹11,641 lakh (up 4% YoY) — a resilient profit performance despite topline pressure - Q1 FY26 (Apr–Jun 2025): Best-ever Q1 in revenue and margins; cranes, construction and material handling volumes up 20% YoY - ACE declared a dividend of ₹2 per share for FY2025-26 (announced May 2026) - Full-year revenue (FY25): ₹3,320 Cr; PAT ₹415 Cr — strong profitability - ICICI Securities maintained a BUY call with target ₹770 (Feb 2026), citing green shoots in solar, irrigation, and rural roads demand, and expectation of meaningful price hikes in FY27 once cash flows normalize - Stock down ~31.7% in 12 months from peak — reflecting broader sector de-rating on domestic demand softness

Investment Characteristics: - Best-in-class return ratios in this peer group: ROCE 31.7%, 5-yr ROE 24.4%, current ROE 22.9% - FCF is strong and consistent — ₹1,102 Cr over 5 years, ₹805 Cr over 3 years - Reasonable P/E at 25.3x; market cap ₹10,502 Cr on ~₹415 Cr PAT - High promoter holding (65.4%) signals confidence; low float can cut both ways - Key risk: Concentrated exposure to crane demand which is directly linked to infrastructure execution pace and contractor payment cycles

2.3 Ajax Engineering Ltd (NSE: AJAX)

Sector: Concreting Solutions — Self-Loading Concrete Mixers (SLCM), Batching Plants, Transit Mixers, Concrete Pumps, Pavers Headquarters: Bengaluru | Founded: 1992 | IPO: February 2025

Ajax Engineering is India's leading player in Self-Loading Concrete Mixers (SLCM), a niche but rapidly growing segment of the construction equipment market. It serves infrastructure, energy/power, and construction sectors. Ajax completed its IPO in February 2025.

Recent Developments (2025–2026): - Post-IPO performance has been volatile — stock down from listing amid broader sector weakness - Reported a volume decline of ~31% in recent quarters (in line with broader industry weakness) - No price hikes implemented — muted pricing environment due to weak demand - ICICI Securities maintained a BUY at ₹770 (Feb 2026) — citing: - Green shoots visible in Feb 2026 volumes on VAHAN data - Demand driven by solar, irrigation, and rural roads segments - Expects meaningful price hikes and margin recovery in FY27 - Despite weakness, sectors like solar power and rural roads are driving concrete equipment demand

Investment Characteristics: - ROCE 23.86%, 5-yr ROE 21.2%, current ROE 17.85% — strong returns for a capital-light niche player - FCF trajectory is concerning: ₹794.61 Cr over 5 years, ₹555.53 Cr over 3 years, but only ₹20.18 Cr in the latest annual — significant FCF compression likely reflecting IPO-related capex and/or working capital build - P/E 28.67x is moderate relative to return ratios but elevated given near-term earnings headwinds - Market cap ₹6,520 Cr; smaller size means higher volatility - Niche leadership in SLCM is a genuine competitive moat but segment is cyclical

2.4 TIL Limited (NSE: TIL)

Sector: Material Handling & Infrastructure Equipment — Cranes, Container Handling, Aerial Work Platforms Headquarters: Kolkata | Founded: 1944 | Parent: Gainwell Group (post-restructuring)

TIL Limited is one of India's oldest construction equipment companies with 8 decades of domain expertise. It manufactures and markets cranes (rough terrain, truck cranes, crawler cranes), container handling equipment, and aerial work platforms. It has strategic partnerships with Grove/Manitowoc (USA), Hyster-Yale Group (container handling), and Snorkel Europe (aerial work platforms for Northern and Eastern India).

Recent Developments (2025–2026): - The company is undergoing a strategic transformation under what it calls "TIL 2.0" under the Gainwell Group - At EXCON 2025 (India's premier CE exhibition, Dec 2025), TIL launched three 100% indigenously built material handling products: - CarryKing 515 — India's first truly safe 15-tonne capacity pick-and-carry-on-deck crane with 360-degree continuous slew - Two other new machines (full details from exhibition) - Production facilities at Kamarhati and Kharagpur declared fully capable of scaling to demand - Positioned to benefit from potential anti-dumping duties on cheap imported cranes - Positive stance on India becoming a manufacturing hub for global CE players

Investment Characteristics: - Negative FCF across all periods: -₹215.56 Cr (5-yr), -₹178.64 Cr (3-yr), -₹50.53 Cr (latest annual) — significant red flag; company has been consuming cash - No P/E available — implies the company may be loss-making or earnings near zero; ROE of 0.91% confirms near-zero profitability - ROCE of 11.08% shows some operational efficiency but return to shareholders has been negligible - Market cap ₹1,764 Cr is the smallest in this set after Brady & Morris - High-risk turnaround story — the "TIL 2.0" narrative is compelling but financials remain unproven - No dividend, negative FCF, minimal ROE — unsuitable for most frameworks without turnaround confirmation

2.5 Brady & Morris Engineering Company Ltd (NSE: BRADYMORR)

Sector: Lifting & Material Handling Equipment — EOT Cranes, Hoists, Goods Lifts, Material Handling Systems Headquarters: Mumbai | Market Cap: ₹207.68 Cr (micro-cap)

Brady & Morris is a specialized, small-scale manufacturer of overhead cranes, electric hoists, goods lifts, and industrial material handling systems. It serves industries like steel, cement, ports, power, and manufacturing. It is a niche B2B supplier rather than a broad construction equipment company.

Recent Developments: - Financially stable relative to peers per Tijori Finance analysis — lower probability of financial distress compared to immediate competitors - Has maintained consistent profitability — P/E 42.64x on a micro-cap base - ROCE 25.4%, 5-yr ROE 33.61%, current ROE 23.36% — the highest return ratios in this peer set on a historical basis - FCF: ₹36.93 Cr (5-yr), ₹29.16 Cr (3-yr), ₹3.38 Cr (last annual) — positive FCF but very small in absolute terms and declining

Investment Characteristics: - Extremely small market cap (₹207 Cr) — highly illiquid, wide bid-ask spreads likely - Return ratios are excellent on a historical basis but the declining FCF trend needs watching - High P/E (42.64x) on a micro-cap with low liquidity is a risk; any earnings disappointment = sharp correction - Niche moat in industrial lifting — not directly exposed to road/highway cycle swings - Not suitable for large portfolios due to liquidity constraints

5. Buy / Watch List / Avoid Recommendation

✅ BUY LIST

🥇 Action Construction Equipment (ACE) — Primary Conviction BUY

  • Rationale: Best combination of return quality (ROCE 31.7%, ROE 24.4%), FCF generation (₹1,102 Cr over 5 years), and reasonable valuation (25.3x P/E). The stock has de-rated ~32% over 12 months, creating an attractive entry point.
  • Catalysts: Recovery in contractor payments from states (particularly post-election); FY27 price hike potential; solar, irrigation, and rural roads driving crane demand; best-ever Q1 FY26 performance shows underlying demand strength.
  • Risk: Continued state payment delays; weak domestic CE demand into FY27 if project awards remain sluggish.
  • Target (analyst consensus): ₹770 (ICICI Securities, Feb 2026); implies ~13% upside from CMP ₹881.90 at time of data capture — but a longer-term view offers considerably more if earnings normalize.
  • Suggested Action: Accumulate at current levels or on dips to ₹780–820 range.

👁️ WATCH LIST

🔵 Ajax Engineering — WATCH with Buy on Dip

  • Rationale: Market leader in Self-Loading Concrete Mixers with strong 5-year return ratios. However, the sharp FCF compression (₹20 Cr vs ₹794 Cr over 5 years) and 31% volume decline make this a "wait and see" situation. Green shoots in VAHAN data (Feb 2026) need to materialize into sustained volume recovery.
  • Trigger to Add: Two consecutive quarters of volume recovery and FCF normalization above ₹100 Cr annually.
  • Risk: If domestic CE demand remains weak through FY27, earnings estimates will see further cuts; post-IPO lock-up expiry selling pressure.

🔵 BEML Ltd — WATCH — Sector-Specific Monitor

  • Rationale: Unique government-backed multi-sector player with real optionality across defence, metro rail, and mining. The new Bengaluru Metro contract and Siemens/STX Engine MoUs show strategic intent. However, P/E at 61x leaves little room for error, and the 5-yr ROE of 7.52% is unimpressive.
  • Trigger to Add: Meaningful execution of metro rail pipeline (Mumbai Metro 4/5/6, Bhubaneswar); privatization or stake-sale newsflow; ROCE sustained above 18%.
  • Risk: Government capex execution is unpredictable; significant overhang from PSU discount; competitive pressure from global players in defence.

🔵 Brady & Morris — WATCH — Micro-Cap Niche Play

  • Rationale: Excellent historical ROE (33.6% over 5 years) and ROCE (25.4%), indicating genuine moat in industrial lifting equipment. Financially stable. BUT: at ₹207 Cr market cap, liquidity is very limited; FCF is declining (₹3.38 Cr last year vs ₹36.93 Cr over 5 years), and P/E at 42.6x is stretched.
  • Trigger to Add: FCF recovery and/or meaningful order wins from steel/power/ports sector; P/E compression to sub-30x.
  • Portfolio Suitability: Only for investors comfortable with micro-cap illiquidity; position sizing must be small.

❌ AVOID

🔴 TIL Limited — AVOID Until Turnaround Confirmed

  • Rationale: Negative FCF across all time horizons (-₹215 Cr over 5 years), near-zero ROE (0.91%), no earnings (P/E not available), and loss-making history. The "TIL 2.0" story and new product launches at EXCON 2025 are encouraging but purely forward-looking.
  • Condition to Revisit: Two consecutive quarters of positive FCF, ROCE above 15%, and demonstrated earnings recovery. Until then, this is a speculative bet, not an investment.

  3. Mumbai Metro Lines 4, 5, 6 + Bhubaneswar Metro — large material handling and lifting equipment demand

  7. Freight corridor completion — Dedicated Freight Corridors Phase 2 driving construction equipment demand in eastern and western corridors
  8. Contractor payment normalization — states clearing dues post-elections will unlock working capital and order execution
