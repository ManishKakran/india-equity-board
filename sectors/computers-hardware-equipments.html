🌐 INDUSTRY CONTEXT — May 2026

Macro Tailwinds

  • India's total IT spending is forecast to reach ₹15.14 lakh crore (~$176.3 billion) in 2026, driven by data-centre expansion and AI-enabled software investment (Gartner, Nov 2025).
  • Software spending in India is projected to grow 17.6% in 2026, reaching $24.7 billion as enterprises ramp up AI and cloud investments.
  • India's installed data-centre capacity is expected to double from 950 MW (2024) to 1,800 MW by 2026, opening new hardware demand corridors.
  • Digital India e-Governance stack expected to award >$3 billion in IT hardware tenders between 2025–27.
  • Computer software & hardware attracted cumulative FDI of ₹8.32 lakh crore (Apr 2000–Jun 2025), ranking 2nd in FDI inflows nationally.

Macro Headwinds

  • Tier-I IT services firms face muted growth due to structural pressures and slowdown in the US market (60% of exports concentrated there).
  • Rapid AI disruption is forcing a transition from legacy service models toward high-end innovation — a risk for companies without R&D capability.
  • Many small hardware distributors face thin margins, high working capital intensity, and negative Free Cash Flow — structural not cyclical issues.

Industry ROCE Benchmark

The Nalanda framework demands median industry ROCE > 12–15% to justify individual stock selection. Based on the peer data provided, the industry median ROCE is approximately 14–17%, with wide dispersion. Several companies have negative ROCE — a structural concern. The industry is borderline acceptable for individual stock selection under this framework.

🏆 CANDIDATE 1 — D-Link India (NSE: DLINKINDIA)

CMP: ₹461 | P/E: 15.74 | Mkt Cap: ₹1,637 Cr

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter/Management Integrity D-Link India is a subsidiary of D-Link Corporation (Taiwan), a Nasdaq-listed global networking brand. Parent company has decades-long track record. No history of fraud, SEBI action, or related-party abuse found. Promoter shareholding is ~51% (D-Link Holding Mauritius Inc.). No pledging reported. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check Company has consistently profitable operations. Not a turnaround story. Revenue ₹1,566 Cr, profit ₹104 Cr (FY25). ROCE has been consistently positive. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.3 — Debt/Leverage Test Net Cash Flow Mar 2025: ₹13.48 Cr. Company operates in a distribution-heavy model with some working capital debt, but overall balance sheet is clean. Working capital days increased from 63.5 to 92.6 days — a flag to monitor. Verdict: PASS (marginal — watch working capital)

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer Check Acquired TeamF1 Networks in 2013–14 (one strategic acquisition). No pattern of serial acquisitions. Organic growth dominates. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change Networking products (routers, switches, surveillance) face technology evolution but not rapid obsolescence. Wi-Fi 7, AI-driven network management are tailwinds, not threats, for D-Link. However, commoditisation risk from Chinese players (TP-Link, Huawei) is real. Verdict: PASS (monitor competitive intensity)

Filter 1.6 — Ownership Alignment Promoter (D-Link Holding Mauritius) holds 51% — adequate skin in game. MNC parent ensures global brand backing and technology access. However, parent's interests may not always align perfectly with minority shareholders. Verdict: PASS

📌 KEY INDUSTRY RISK FLAGS (Darwin Perspective)

  1. Working Capital Trap: Most hardware distributors (Rashi, GNG, DC Infotech) show profits but negative FCF — a classic sign that earnings are funding working capital growth, not real wealth creation.

  2. Commoditisation Risk: Chinese IT hardware brands continue to undercut Indian distributors on price. D-Link faces TP-Link; Rashi faces margin compression.

  3. AI Industry Disruption: Semiconductor/software companies like Moschip face structural disruption risk — exciting as a story, but disqualified by Darwin's industry pace-of-change filter.

  4. Micro-cap Illiquidity: Companies 20–28 (market caps ₹15–40 Cr) are essentially unanalysable under Darwin standards — no track record, no liquidity, no institutional scrutiny.

  5. One Genuine BUY in 28 companies: Only D-Link India clears all Darwin gates at current price. This is consistent with the philosophy — great businesses at fair prices are rare, and the framework is designed to reject most opportunities.
