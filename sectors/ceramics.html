PART I — INDUSTRY ANALYSIS (Outside View / Convergence Test)

"Before picking an individual stock, look at the industry from the outside. If the median industry ROCE is below 12–15%, you need a compelling structural reason why your pick is different — and why that difference is durable."

1.1 Industry Overview

India is the world's second-largest ceramic tile producer and consumer, behind China. The domestic market is large, growing, and structurally driven by long-term tailwinds.

Key Market Facts (Latest Data, 2025–26): - Market size: ~USD 10.45–11.30 billion in 2025–26, expected to reach USD 16–17 billion by 2031 at a CAGR of ~8% (Mordor Intelligence, Feb 2026) - India's domestic consumption: ~1,256 million sq. meters (2025); targeting ~2.2 billion sqm by 2029 - Production capacity: ~1.3 billion sq. meters annually; India is the 2nd-largest manufacturing hub globally - Morbi, Gujarat dominates with 800+ manufacturing units contributing ~70% of national output - Organised sector share: ~46% in FY25, projected to reach 55% by FY29

Demand Drivers (Structural & Durable): - Urbanisation: 40% of India's population expected in urban areas by 2030 - PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana): Millions of affordable housing units accelerating tile demand - Rising middle-class incomes → preference for premium/glazed/porcelain formats - Infrastructure: Smart cities, metro rail corridors, IT parks, hospitality - Renovation cycle: Urban replacement demand growing alongside new construction - Rural penetration: Ceramic tiles displacing traditional flooring in tier-3/4 India

Structural Risks: - Natural gas price volatility (30–35% of variable costs); Gujarat Gas raised industrial prices 8–12% in July 2024 - ~70% of manufacturers are unorganised (primarily Morbi), creating intense low-end price competition - Export headwinds: Anti-dumping duties in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Taiwan; US export volumes fell 14.7% in 2025 (Jan–Sep); Mexico launched anti-dumping investigation (August 2025) - Online direct-to-consumer channels growing at ~14.49% CAGR, compressing distributor margins - Shifting to large-format porcelain slabs (20–30% price premium) favours organised players

PART IV — KEY INDUSTRY INSIGHTS FOR THE LONG-TERM INVESTOR

Why the industry is hard (but Kajaria is worth watching):

The Indian ceramics sector is structurally a difficult industry for capital allocation. Nine out of ten listed players earn ROCE below 15%; six earn below 10%. The fundamental challenge is the Morbi unorganised cluster — 800+ small manufacturers who produce at low cost and low quality, perpetually capping pricing power for mid-tier organised players. Only those with true brand equity and distribution superiority (i.e., Kajaria) can price 20% above Morbi and retain customers.

The one durable structural trend that matters: Organised sector share is growing from 46% (FY25) → projected 55% (FY29). In a ~₹85,000+ Cr annual market, this shift of ~9 percentage points represents ₹7,000–8,000 Cr of incremental revenue migrating from unorganised to organised over 4 years. Kajaria, as the dominant organised player at 17% share, captures a disproportionate slice of this migration.

Risks that are real and durable (not just proximate): 1. Natural gas price volatility — a 10% gas price rise directly erodes 3–3.5% of gross margins and is beyond management control 2. Export market compression — the US (historically the largest export destination) saw a 14.7% volume decline in 2025; Mexico anti-dumping investigation adds to uncertainty 3. Competition from global entrants — Mohawk Industries (US) acquired a local Indian manufacturer (Sep 2025), signalling global players are entering India's growing market

The bottom line: In 10 companies, the Darwin framework identifies exactly one worth owning — Kajaria Ceramics — and even that only at the right price. This is precisely what the framework predicts about industries with low median ROCE: extraordinary winners are rare, and the majority are capital-destroying traps in various disguises (some cheap-looking, some story-driven, some briefly profitable). Patient investors should wait for Kajaria to reach the ₹650–750 range before initiating a position.
