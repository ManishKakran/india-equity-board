A.1 Industry Classification

The nine companies span two related industrial sub-sectors:

Sub-Sector Companies
Bearings Timken India, SKF India (Industrial), SKF India, NRB Bearings (Indl), SKP Bearing, Galaxy Bearings
Abrasives & Precision Tools Carborundum Universal (CUMI), Grindwell Norton, Wendt India

These are precision engineering consumables and components — not commodities, not technology. They occupy the invisible load-bearing infrastructure of all manufacturing. Every motor, vehicle, turbine, and machine tool uses either bearings or abrasives. This is critical industrial plumbing.

A.2 Industry Structure & Economics

Bearings — India Market (Latest 2025–26 Data) - Market size: ~USD 5.2 Billion (2025); projected USD 12 Billion by 2034 — CAGR ~9.7% - Key demand drivers: Automotive (~50% of demand, two-wheelers dominant), Industrial Machinery (~30%), Railways & Aerospace (emerging) - Organized vs. Unorganised: Top 5 global players (SKF, Schaeffler, Timken, NSK, FAG) dominate organised segment; significant unorganized counterfeit market in aftermarket - EV Transition Impact: Neutral-to-positive for specialised bearing makers. EVs require fewer but more precise bearings; demand shifts from high-volume commodity to precision-engineered. Wind turbines (one of India's fastest growing energy segments) are highly bearing-intensive - Key structural risks: Chinese low-cost imports (counterfeit and legitimate), steel price volatility (~40% of bearing cost is steel)

Abrasives — India Market (Latest 2025–26 Data) - Market size: ~₹5,000 crore (~USD 2.2 Billion) domestic (2025); CAGR ~6% till 2033 - Products: Bonded abrasives (wheels, segments) + Coated abrasives (belts, discs) + Superabrasives (CBN, diamond — fastest growing, ~15% CAGR) - Key demand drivers: Metal fabrication, automotive OEM, construction, aerospace/defence, Make in India push - Organised sector: CUMI (~30% market share, India's largest) and Grindwell Norton (Saint-Gobain subsidiary) together control the quality segment - Chinese competition: ~30% of premium abrasives imports; domestic players face margin pressure in conventional segments but have pricing power in high-performance/specialised categories - Government push: Import restrictions on some foreign abrasives, PLI-adjacent benefits for domestic manufacturing

Industry Attractiveness Verdict (Darwin "Outside View" / Convergence Test):

Parameter Assessment
Pace of technological change SLOW — Bearings and abrasives are 100+ year-old technologies. Core physics unchanged
Ease of new entrant displacing incumbents DIFFICULT — Precision manufacturing, MNC parentage, distribution moats, qualification cycles
Median ROCE of top 5 industry players ~18–22% for Grindwell Norton, Timken, SKF Industrial. Dragged down by smaller players
Industry median ROCE ~12–15% (marginal pass for Darwin's ≥12% industry bar)
Structural threats Chinese imports (manageable), EV transition (manageable), steel inflation (cyclical)
Secular demand story STRONG — Industrialisation, infrastructure, PLI manufacturing build-out, railways, wind energy

Bottom Line: This is a Rainforest industry for the Darwin framework — slow-changing, structurally sound, incumbent-friendly. The outside view supports picking individual winners. The industry can produce high-ROCE businesses, as proven by Grindwell Norton and SKF Industrial.

🏆 COMPANY 1: GRINDWELL NORTON LTD

CMP: ₹1,859.90 | P/E: 49.54x | Mkt Cap: ₹20,592 Cr | ROCE: 22.71%

Saint-Gobain India's abrasives, refractories, and performance plastics arm. India's #2 abrasives manufacturer.

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter/Management Integrity ✅ PASS Grindwell Norton is a subsidiary of Compagnie de Saint-Gobain (France), one of the world's oldest industrial companies (founded 1665). The Indian entity has operated for decades under consistent French MNC governance. No history of SEBI actions, fraud, or related-party abuse. Clean regulatory record. Statutory auditors have consistently issued unmodified opinions, confirmed again in Q4 FY26.

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check ✅ PASS Not a turnaround. The company has delivered consistent profits for 10+ consecutive years. FY25 was a period of mild growth deceleration (revenue +4.7% YoY, margins contracted slightly) but this was an industry-wide abrasives cycle slowdown, not a structural failure. Q4 FY26 results (revenue +18.7% YoY, PAT +28.3% YoY, operating margins 19.58%) confirm the underlying business is intact and accelerating.

Filter 1.3 — Debt/Leverage Test ✅ PASS Grindwell Norton is virtually debt-free. FCF has been consistently positive across 5-year (₹1,127.92 Cr) and 3-year (₹989.38 Cr) horizons. The 5-year FCF generation of ₹1,127 Cr against a market cap of ~₹20,000 Cr reflects a capital-light, high-quality business. Interest coverage is well above 10x. No debt dependency for growth.

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer Check ✅ PASS Saint-Gobain in India has grown organically. Grindwell Norton has not been a serial acquirer. Product expansion (ceramics, refractories, performance plastics, abrasives) has been internally developed or through parent technology transfer, not M&A roll-ups. No goodwill concern.

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change ✅ PASS Abrasives are one of the most stable industrial product categories. The core chemistry (aluminium oxide, silicon carbide, CBN) has been stable for decades. Superabrasives are an incremental evolution, not a disruption. No technology has threatened to obsolete abrasive grinding in the foreseeable future. Grindwell Norton itself has thrived for 70+ years without a single "punctuation event."

Filter 1.6 — Ownership Alignment ⚠️ CONDITIONAL PASS Saint-Gobain (France) is the promoter with ~51%+ stake. This is an MNC subsidiary structure, not a founder-entrepreneur model. The Darwin framework ideally wants an entrepreneur-owner. However, Saint-Gobain's 360-year operating history, strategic long-term orientation, and continuous technology infusion from the parent makes this an alignment-compatible structure. Saint-Gobain has demonstrated excellent capital allocation in India — no equity dilution, consistent dividends, no value-destructive transactions. The spirit of the filter (long-term skin in the game) is met.

🥈 COMPANY 2: TIMKEN INDIA LTD

CMP: ₹3,531.20 | P/E: 61.72x | Mkt Cap: ₹26,561 Cr | ROCE: 20.93%

India's only specialty tapered roller bearing manufacturer. Subsidiary of The Timken Company (USA). Also makes Alloy steel through a manufacturing JV.

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter/Management Integrity ⚠️ CONDITIONAL PASS The Timken Company (USA) is the promoter (~75% holding). No history of fraud or SEBI action. However, two concerns emerged in FY26: 1. IT Department Tax Order: Income Tax demand of ₹747.7 crore issued related to transfer pricing methodology — a material amount (~2.8% of market cap). The company has appealed. Transfer pricing disputes in MNC subsidiaries are common in India and don't necessarily indicate integrity issues, but the quantum is significant and must be monitored. 2. Management transitions: CEO Srinivasan Sarangapani moved to a global role; CFO Avishrant Keshava resigned effective April 30, 2026. Back-to-back senior exits require monitoring.

The transfer pricing issue is a regulatory/tax matter, not fraud, and the company has disclosed it promptly. Passing with caution flagged.

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check ✅ PASS Not a turnaround. Timken India has been profitable for 10+ consecutive years. Q3 FY26 profit drop (41.71%) was due to identifiable one-offs: ₹750 crore Bharuch plant ramp-up costs (170 bps PBT impact), transfer pricing provisions, and GGB Technology integration costs. Revenue grew 13.8% in Q3 FY26. This is a capacity investment cycle, not a business deterioration.

Filter 1.3 — Debt/Leverage Test ✅ PASS Near-zero debt (D/E ~0.55% per Yahoo Finance data). 5-year FCF: ₹438 Cr. 3-year FCF: ₹374 Cr. FCF is lower than Grindwell Norton (due to higher capex intensity for bearing manufacturing vs. abrasives), but comfortably positive. The ₹750 Cr Bharuch capex was funded internally — no debt raised.

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer Check ⚠️ FLAG Timken India made the GGB Technology acquisition (FY26) — a plain bearings/polymer bearings business contributing ~₹50–55 Cr annual revenue. This is one acquisition in the last 5 years. Not serial. However, the acquisition appears small and strategic (entering adjacent plain bearings market). The GGB entity had ₹19.5 Cr PBT on ₹50 Cr revenue (39% margin) — excellent economics. Passes, but the flag is noted.

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change ✅ PASS Specialty tapered roller bearings are among the most durable product categories. Timken's 125-year history proves longevity. EV transition creates demand for new bearing variants (EV drivetrains, e-axles) — Timken globally is positioned for this evolution, and benefits are flowing to the India entity. Rail bearings (a growth segment) is insulated from EV disruption entirely.

Filter 1.6 — Ownership Alignment ⚠️ CONDITIONAL PASS The Timken Company (USA) holds ~75% — the highest promoter stake in the universe. Deep skin in the game. Not a founder in the Indian entrepreneurial sense, but the parent's alignment is unquestionable. Passing.
