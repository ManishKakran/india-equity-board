🌐 INDUSTRY DEEP DIVE: Indian Aviation Sector

Sector Overview (May 2026)

Indian aviation is one of the most structurally promising yet operationally brutal industries in the world. The contrast is stark: enormous long-run growth tailwinds colliding with thin margins, regulatory complexity, fuel cost volatility, and a graveyard of failed carriers.

Scale & Growth: - India is the 3rd-largest domestic aviation market globally, behind the US and China - Domestic passenger traffic: 166.9 million in 2025 (just 3.5% YoY growth — a deceleration from prior years) - International traffic (Apr 2024–Jan 2025): 28.09 million, up 41.3% vs pre-pandemic levels - Airport network: from 74 airports in 2014 → 163 by October 2025 - Market size: USD ~16.5 billion in 2026, projected to reach ~USD 29 billion by 2031 at ~12% CAGR - India expected to surpass China in passenger traffic growth by 2026 (Jefferies: 10.5% vs 8.9%) - Per capita air travel remains among the lowest globally — significant headroom vs China

Structural Tailwinds: - Rising middle class and disposable incomes - UDAN scheme expanding regional connectivity to Tier-2/3 cities - Government target: 400 airports by 2047 with ₹98,000 crore infrastructure investment - Fleet projected to reach 1,100 aircraft by 2027 - MRO industry expected to reach $4 billion by 2031

Structural Headwinds (Critical for Nalanda Framework): - Fuel not under GST — airlines pay full taxes, cannot claim input credits on their largest cost item - Extreme price sensitivity of Indian passengers - Rupee depreciation risk — leases, parts, maintenance all USD-denominated; revenue in INR - Supply chain disruptions — aircraft delivery backlogs averaging 9.7 years industry-wide - FDTL regulatory shocks (late 2025) — DGCA implemented stricter pilot duty/rest norms, causing mass cancellations at IndiGo in December 2025 - Market concentration risk — HHI ~4,500; one carrier (IndiGo) controls ~62% - Historical graveyard: Kingfisher, Jet Airways, Air Deccan — all collapsed under debt

Industry-Level ROCE (The "Convergence Test"): This is the single most important data point from the Nalanda framework applied at the industry level.

The aviation services/niche sub-sectors (MRO, charter, helicopter logistics) show more differentiated economics. The framework strongly cautions against investing in industries where structural returns are below the cost of capital.

STOCK 1: INTERGLOBE AVIATION (IndiGo) | NSE: INDIGO

CMP: ₹4,314.90 | P/E: 36.81 | Mkt Cap: ₹1,66,838 Cr

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

FILTER 1.1 — PROMOTER / MANAGEMENT INTEGRITY

Assessment: - Rahul Bhatia (InterGlobe Enterprises) is the founding promoter and Group MD. He retains board control and a meaningful equity stake. - Rakesh Gangwal (co-founder) has been systematically reducing his stake since 2022 following a governance dispute with Bhatia over board rights and transparency. By early 2025, Gangwal family entities hold ~13.53% (down from ~37% at IPO). - The governance dispute was a yellow flag but was ultimately resolved without regulatory censure or fraud allegations. - No SEBI enforcement actions or fraud findings against the company or promoters. - Promoter (Bhatia group) stake: approximately 37–38% as of FY25; Gangwal ~13.5%. Combined promoter bloc is still dominant. - No significant pledging of promoter shares reported. - December 2025 operational crisis triggered a DGCA show-cause notice to CEO Pieter Elbers and a ₹22.2 crore fine. This is regulatory, not fraudulent.

Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS — Governance dispute history warrants monitoring, but no fraud, no pledging, management stake remains meaningful. The Gangwal exit removes a potential corporate governance overhang.

FILTER 1.2 — TURNAROUND CHECK

Assessment: - IndiGo is NOT a turnaround story. It has been consistently profitable since FY11 (with a COVID exception in FY21-22). - FY25: Passengers +11.1% to 118.6 million; capacity +13.1%; strong FCF generation of ₹20,780 Cr in the last annual period. - The investment thesis is built on demonstrated historical excellence, not hope. - The December 2025 FDTL crisis was an operational disruption, not a structural collapse. ICRA and Moody's flagged it as manageable.

Verdict: PASS

FILTER 1.3 — DEBT / LEVERAGE TEST

Assessment — CRITICAL FAILURE POINT: - IndiGo carries massive lease liabilities under Ind AS 116 (IFRS 16). Total debt as of March 2025: approximately $7.81 billion USD (≈₹65,000+ crore) including lease liabilities. - Borrowings (ex-leases): ~₹7,800 crore vs cash of ~₹34,737 crore (FY24). Cash comfortably exceeds financial debt. - However, lease liabilities of ₹43,488 crore (FY24) represent real, inescapable obligations — these are not optional. Under the Nalanda framework, lease liabilities must be treated as debt. - Interest coverage on a gross basis is not "10x+" once lease interest is included. - ICRA warned in December 2025 that "financial leverage (Net debt including lease liabilities minus free cash / EBITDAR) is likely to breach its negative trigger of 2.5x in FY2026." - The business model is intrinsically dependent on operating leases — this is structural, not temporary.

The Nalanda Debt Test is PRECISE: D/E ≤ 0.3, interest coverage > 10x, not reliant on debt for growth. IndiGo fails this emphatically when leases are correctly included as obligations.

Verdict: ❌ DISQUALIFY — Filter 1.3 FAILED Reason: Enormous lease liabilities (~₹43,000–65,000+ crore) represent structural financial obligations that are inseparable from the airline business model. These are real obligations to aircraft lessors — the Jet Airways and Kingfisher collapses were triggered by exactly this leverage dynamic. Even with strong cash buffers, the D/E (including leases) is multiple times above the Nalanda maximum of 0.3. The ICRA warning about leverage breaching negative triggers in FY26 further confirms this.

⛔ GATE 1 SUMMARY — DISQUALIFIED at Filter 1.3

No further analysis required under the Darwin framework.

One-Line Thesis (Why NOT): IndiGo is an operationally excellent, market-dominant airline, but the Darwin framework demands zero/minimal debt as a non-negotiable filter; IndiGo's structural dependence on tens of thousands of crores of lease obligations — the precise liability structure that has destroyed every other Indian airline — makes it uninvestable under a permanent capital framework, regardless of the growth story.

📝 INVESTOR NOTE (Beyond the Framework):

However, the P/E of 36.81x is far above Nalanda's preferred entry (14.9x historically) and even above the Sensex TTM PE (~22–24x in 2026). Even in a liberal reading, IndiGo would land as a WATCHLIST at best — monitor for entry at ₹2,800–3,000 (20–25% lower) if the lease concern is set aside.
