PART 1 — INDUSTRY ANALYSIS (Outside View / Convergence Test)

"One of our principles of convergence investing is that if the industry allows its companies to make money consistently, we love it; if not, we better have a perfect reason for spending even one minute analysing a business." — Pulak Prasad

1.1 What Does the Industry Produce?

The Electrodes & Refractories sector has two distinct sub-segments with very different economics:

Sub-segment A — Graphite Electrodes Used exclusively in Electric Arc Furnaces (EAF) for steelmaking. A consumable product — melts away with each heat cycle. Primary raw material: needle coke (petroleum-derived), which accounts for ~60% of production cost. Only four companies globally can produce Ultra High Power (UHP) grade electrodes at scale (Graftech, Showa Denko, Tokai Carbon, HEG/Graphite India in India). Technology is proprietary, capital-intensive, and not easily replicated.

Sub-segment B — Refractories Heat-resistant ceramic materials lining furnaces, kilns, and reactors across steel, cement, glass, and non-ferrous metals industries. Products range from bricks and monolithics to foundry consumables (Foseco's specialty). More fragmented industry — many domestic and global players. Steel accounts for ~65% of global refractory demand.

1.2 Industry Structural Attractiveness (2025–2026 Update)

Graphite Electrode Industry: - Global market size: ~$4.7 billion (2026); growing at 6.6% CAGR to $7.85 billion by 2034 - Volume: 1.78 million tonnes in 2026, projected 2.13 million tonnes by 2031 at 3.63% CAGR - India steel production grew 10.2% YoY in 2025 — fastest among major nations; demand projected +9% in 2026 - The global shift toward EAF-based steelmaking (lower CO₂ than blast furnace) is a secular tailwind — EAF accounts for ~30% of global steel; expected to rise materially - Severe pricing headwind (2024–2026): Aggressive Chinese export dumping has suppressed global electrode prices. UHP electrode prices in India were $2,678/MT in June 2025 vs. ~$10,000+/MT during the 2018 super-cycle. Margin pressure is intense for Indian producers. - March 2026 development: US-based Graftech announced a $600–$1,200/MT price hike. ICICI Securities flagged this as a positive sentiment signal for HEG and Graphite India. However, brokerages are awaiting confirmation that Indian players can implement similar hikes and whether needle coke cost increases offset gains. - Convergence verdict on graphite electrodes: The industry can generate excellent returns (2018 super-cycle: EBITDA margins of 58% for Graphite India). But returns are highly cyclical and structurally dependent on Chinese pricing behaviour and needle coke costs. This is a Convergence Type 3 industry: only some companies make money; only the undisputed leaders (HEG, Graphite India) have cost/technology advantages that provide any buffer.

Refractories Industry (India): - India refractories market: $2.6 billion (2025); projected $4.5 billion by 2030 at 8.3% CAGR (Grand View Research) - Steel remains the dominant end-user (65% share); cement and glass are secondary drivers - The sector is benefiting from India's infrastructure push, PLI schemes, and steel capacity expansion - RHI Magnesita's CEO explicitly forecasts 6–13% annual refractory demand growth in India - Challenges: alumina and magnesite price surges; currency depreciation pressuring margins; Chinese dumping of commoditised refractories - Convergence verdict on refractories: The industry structurally allows companies to earn decent returns, especially niche/specialised players. Iron & steel segment retains highest CAGR at 4.29% through 2031. This is a Convergence Type 2 industry for niche players: industry is broadly attractive, and leaders with strong technical/customer moats (Vesuvius, Foseco) earn consistently above cost of capital.

1.3 Industry Pace of Change

Both sub-segments score well on the Nalanda "slow-changing industry" filter: - Graphite electrode technology has been stable for decades; no known substitute at EAF scale - Refractory chemistry changes slowly; customer relationships are long-term (furnace relining contracts are multi-year) - EAF steelmaking is not at risk of obsolescence — it is the green direction - No digital disruption risk - Assessment: SLOW-CHANGING INDUSTRY — passes Filter 1.5

PART 4 — INDUSTRY INVESTMENT THESIS SUMMARY

What the Darwin Framework tells us about this sector:

  1. The graphite electrode sub-segment (Graphite India, HEG) is a classic commodity cyclical — capable of generating supernormal ROCE in peaks (2018: 40–58% EBITDA margins) but chronically below threshold in troughs. The Nalanda framework would only engage after 5+ years of sustained ROCE >20%, which requires cycle recovery AND structural pricing improvement. The March 2026 Graftech price hike announcement is a potential inflection point — worth monitoring but not acting on.

  2. The refractory sub-segment has better structural ROCE characteristics — niche players with proprietary products and strong customer relationships (Vesuvius, Foseco, Raghav) consistently earn 20–35% ROCE. These pass the convergence test and are the businesses the Darwin framework would own.

  3. India's infrastructure cycle (2025–2030) is a genuine tailwind — steel production growing 10.2% YoY, refractory market projected to grow at 8.3% CAGR, cement and glass also expanding. This confirms the industry "structurally allows companies to earn returns above cost of capital" — but only the niche leaders, not the commoditised players.

  4. Raw material volatility is the key industry risk — needle coke for electrodes; alumina, magnesite for refractories. Both have seen price surges and supply disruptions. Companies with pricing power over customers (proprietary products) handle this better than commodity suppliers.

  5. China's role as disruptor: Chinese oversupply of commodity electrodes and refractories keeps pricing suppressed for undifferentiated products. Differentiated players (Foseco, Vesuvius, Raghav) are somewhat insulated; commodity players (HEG, Graphite India in current cycle) are not.
