PART I — INDUSTRY OUTSIDE VIEW (The Convergence / Darwin Test)

Before picking any individual stock, the Darwin framework demands an "outside view" — what does the industry base rate say? Only industries with median peer ROCE > 12% are worth hunting in.

Industry Overview: Indian Cement (May 2025 Update)

India is the world's second-largest cement producer and consumer. The structural demand story is intact and strengthening:

  • Production: FY25 output reached 453 MT, up 6.3% YoY. Output in Apr–Jul FY26 grew 8.9% YoY.
  • Demand growth: FY26 expected to grow 6.5–7.5%, driven by the ₹102 lakh crore National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP), PMAY housing, and dedicated freight corridor projects.
  • Installed capacity: ~668–690 MTPA as of March 2025; set to reach 850 MTPA by 2030.
  • Capacity utilisation: ~70% in FY25 (vs. 10-year average of 65%), suggesting improved pricing power.
  • Operating profit: ICRA forecasts 12–18% rise in sector EBITDA/MT in FY26.
  • Capex supercycle: Top 17 producers plan ₹1,20,000 crore in capex between FY26–FY28 — 50% higher than the prior three years.
  • Consolidation: UltraTech, Adani (Ambuja + ACC), and Shree are rapidly consolidating. Top 3 players now control ~55%+ of capacity.
  • Per capita consumption: ~290 kg vs. global average of 540 kg — significant headroom for growth.
  • Green cement: Sustainability is becoming a structural capex item, not merely compliance.
  • Key risks: Energy/fuel cost volatility, regional oversupply (especially South India at ~60% utilisation), aggressive capacity addition could pressure pricing in FY27–28.

PART V — INDUSTRY RISKS & DARWIN FRAMEWORK CAUTIONS

Structural Risks for the Indian Cement Sector (2025–2028)

  1. Capacity overhang: 160–170 MT of new capacity being added FY26–28 (75% jump vs. prior 3 years). Utilisation could dip back to 65% or below, pressuring pricing and ROCE for all players.

  2. Energy cost volatility: Coal and pet coke prices remain the single biggest earnings risk. No structural hedge exists for most players.

  3. Adani-Aditya Birla duopoly risk: As the top 2 groups control 55%+ of capacity, smaller players face existential pressure on pricing in shared geographies.

  4. South India weakness: At 60–62% utilisation, South India (where Orient Cement and Heidelberg operate) is the most vulnerable region. This is the primary risk to the BUY recommendation on Orient.

  5. Green transition capex: Decarbonisation investments (waste heat recovery, green power, carbon capture) require substantial outlay that will consume FCF for the next decade.

Darwin Principle Reminders

  • The Siberian Fox Test: ROCE is the single trait that brings along all good business qualities. No cement company in India currently clears the 20% ROCE bar — this is why the sector naturally yields WATCHLIST and MONITOR rather than strong BUYs.
  • The Bumblebee Test: The 10–15 companies at the bottom of this list (Shiva, Sri Chakra, Panyam, Andhra, Keerthi) are genuinely dangerous to capital. Avoiding them is as important as finding the good ones.
  • The Cicada Test: Once Orient Cement is bought, the instruction is to do almost nothing. Do not sell on P/E re-rating. Do not trade around quarterly results. Own it for the decade.
