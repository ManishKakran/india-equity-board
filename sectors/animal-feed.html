INDUSTRY OVERVIEW — AQUACULTURE & ANIMAL FEED (India)

Market Size & Growth

India's overall animal feed market was valued at USD 14.5 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 21 billion by 2031 at a CAGR of ~6.4%. Within this, aquaculture feed is the fastest-growing sub-segment, projected to expand at a 9.12% CAGR through 2031. The aquafeed market specifically reached USD 3.25–4.03 billion in 2024–25 and could reach USD 5.9 billion by 2033.

Key Structural Tailwinds

  • India is the world's second-largest aquaculture producer and a top-three shrimp exporter globally
  • Government's Blue Revolution 2.0 initiative promoting shrimp farming, pond aeration subsidies, and hatchery modernisation
  • Per capita fish consumption rose to 5.7 kg in 2025, driven by urban consumers shifting to lean protein
  • Shrimp accounts for 75% of India's total marine export value (~$7.5B in FY25)
  • Shift from extensive to intensive aquaculture is structurally increasing commercial feed dependency
  • Organised retail seafood is growing, requiring consistent-quality produce → better feed

Critical Risk Factor: US Tariff Shock (2025–26)

This is the industry's defining proximate-cause event. The US imposed a 50% combined tariff on Indian shrimp (25% reciprocal + 25% penalty for India buying Russian oil), stacked over existing anti-dumping duties of 2.49% and CVD of 5.77% → effective duty of 58.26%. The US absorbed ~48% of India's $5B shrimp exports in FY25. Crisil projects a 15–18% drop in export volumes and 18–20% revenue decline for exporters in FY26.

Critically for feed makers: Feed companies like Avanti are better cushioned than exporters because they benefit from stable domestic demand. The export disruption hits processors and farmers, not feed makers directly (farmers still need feed for domestic-market shrimp and alternative species). Avanti has expanded its premium processing lines at Krishnapatnam to hedge this.

INVESTMENT ACTIONS

⏳ WATCHLIST: AVANTI FEEDS (AVANTIFEED)

One-line thesis: Avanti Feeds deserves a place in a permanent portfolio because it is India's dominant shrimp feed manufacturer with improving ROCE (26.4% in FY25), zero debt, consistent FCF, and a 43% promoter-aligned ownership, serving a structurally growing aquaculture market — the current entry price at 28.5x TTM PE is too rich; a 30–35% correction to the ₹780–900 range would offer the required margin of safety.

Watch trigger: Price below ₹900 = begin research; Price below ₹800 = strong buy candidate under this framework. Risk to monitor: Persistence of US tariff headwinds on shrimp farmers (reduces feed demand); disease outbreaks in Andhra Pradesh ponds; raw material cost spikes (fishmeal, soybean).

📋 MONITOR: KSE LIMITED (KSE)

Note: KSE's 42.92% ROCE is extraordinary — the highest in this peer group and close to Nalanda's portfolio median of ~42%. At just 5.35x PE it appears deeply undervalued. The concern is purely structural: promoter holding at 22.9% fails the Nalanda ownership alignment test. This is a cooperative-style company, not founder-entrepreneur led.

For the pragmatic investor: If you are comfortable with the ownership structure (Kerala cooperative background, 60+ years of institutional trust), KSE at 5.35x PE with 42.92% ROCE, near-zero debt, and positive FCF is a genuinely interesting deep-value situation. Monitor closely.

Watch trigger: Research the ownership history — if promoter holding is stable and governance is clean, this warrants a full Gate 1/Gate 2 analysis.

KEY INDUSTRY RISKS TO TRACK (2026)

  1. US–India trade deal progress — any tariff reduction would be a significant positive catalyst for shrimp exporters AND feed makers (creates demand uplift). Recent reports suggest US–India trade talks are ongoing.
  2. Disease outbreaks — Andhra Pradesh shrimp farms have faced WSSV, EHP, and KHV, reducing cultivated area from 6 lakh acres to 1 lakh acres. This is a cyclical risk that suppresses feed demand temporarily.
  3. Ecuador competition — Ecuador faces only 15% effective US tariff vs India's 58%. If India's shrimp loses US market share permanently (not just cyclically), shrimp feed demand could structurally shift.
  4. Fishmeal import costs — Input cost volatility from Peru fishmeal (global benchmark) directly impacts Avanti's and Mukka's margins.
  5. Value-added shrimp pivot — Indian processors pivoting to ready-to-cook (value-added exports surged 27% to 49,691 MT in H1 2025) could partially offset volume losses. Avanti's Krishnapatnam processing expansion is aligned with this trend.
