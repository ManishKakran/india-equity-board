84. Industrial Minerals Companies — Darwin Investor Framework Analysis

Date of Analysis: June 25, 2026 Analyst Framework: Pulak Prasad / Nalanda Capital Methodology Companies Screened: 15 Current Sensex / Nifty50 TTM PE: ~20.5–21x Darwin Target Entry PE: 14.4–15.4x (25–30% discount to index PE) Nalanda Documented Median Entry: 14.9x

SECTOR OVERVIEW

The Indian Industrial Minerals sector as classified by screeners is a heterogeneous universe spanning five distinct business archetypes:

  1. Iron Ore / Manganese Mining — Lloyds Metals (private), NMDC, MOIL (PSU)
  2. Multi-Mineral Solutions (bentonite, bauxite, kaolin) — GMDC (PSU), Ashapura Minechem (private)
  3. Lead / Non-Ferrous Metal Recycling — Gravita India, NILE (both private)
  4. Carbon / Calcined Petroleum Coke — Goa Carbon, Petro Carbon
  5. Specialty Industrial Minerals — 20 Microns, Southern Magnesium, Orissa Minerals, Asi Industries, Raw Edge, Kachchh Minerals

Sector headwinds relevant to the Darwin framework: - Heavy capex requirements in mining mean persistent negative FCF even for good operators - Government dominates large-scale mineral reserves (iron ore, manganese, bauxite) - Input commodity price volatility causes ROCE cyclicality - Environmental regulations, mining lease renewals, and Naxal-area sensitivities add non-business risk - Lead-acid battery demand (key end market for recyclers) faces long-term EV headwinds, though 10+ year runway in India remains intact for two-wheelers, telecom towers, and UPS

INDIVIDUAL COMPANY ANALYSES — PRIVATE SECTOR

1. LLOYDS METALS AND ENERGY LTD

CMP: ₹1,704.30 | PE: 30.07x | Market Cap: ₹95,936 Cr | ROCE: 35.86%

Business Description

Lloyds Metals operates iron ore mines in Gadchiroli district, Maharashtra (Surjagarh blocks — 157 MNT DSO reserves + 706 MNT BHQ reserves), alongside sponge iron manufacturing and power generation. The company is executing a ₹32,700 Cr cumulative capex plan (FY23–FY31) to become an integrated steel manufacturer — adding 4 MTPA pellet plants, a 1.2 MTPA EAF wire rod mill (FY26), and a 3 MTPA Consteel HRC plant (FY28). In FY25 alone, capex was ₹3,695 Cr. The company also acquired an 83% stake in Thriveni Earthmovers Private Limited (MDO operator) for ₹70 Cr, adding contract mining capacity across India and Indonesia.

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter / Management Integrity

  • Promoter holding: 63.73% (Bajaj family / Thriveni group joint promoters since FY2022)
  • Promoter pledge: 14.42% of promoter shares pledged (Dec 2025), with partial release in progress. As of June 18, 2026, Crosslink Food and Farms (a promoter entity) released 22 lakh pledged shares to Bajaj Finance and Tata Capital.
  • Governance flag — June 2026 AGM: At the company's 49th AGM on June 19, 2026, institutional investors showed "significant pushback on related party transactions" (RPTs). All 22 resolutions were passed but with notable institutional dissent on RPT approvals — a credible governance warning.
  • Historical contract breach: In Q1 FY2023, Lloyds paid a ₹1,194 Cr non-operating loss (net ₹930 Cr) after breaking its ore-supply contract with Sunflag Iron & Steel (which had advanced ₹312 Cr for mine development).
  • Operations are in Naxal-affected Gadchiroli — adds operational and governance complexity.

Assessment: The combination of promoter share pledging, institutional dissent on RPTs, and historical contract breach creates a credible governance concern. The framework's explicit warning — "Nalanda lost $50M at Warburg Puncus by ignoring informal buzz — never again" — applies here. This is not a confirmed fraud, but the governance trajectory warrants a CAUTION flag that weighs significantly against Gate 1 passage.

Filter 1.1 Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS — governance flags noted; requires ongoing monitoring

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check The company has been operationally profitable in mining for several years. The iron ore mine is not a turnaround — it is a functioning asset with genuine economics. However, the transformation into integrated steel (pellets → DRI → wire rod → HRC) represents a complete business model change, with first steel revenues from wire rod mill expected only in FY26–27. Filter 1.2 Verdict: PASS (mining operations established; steel transformation is expansion, not turnaround)

Filter 1.3 — Debt / Leverage - Debt: ~₹746 Cr; Cash: ~₹739 Cr → Net debt ~₹7 Cr (net neutral) - D/E ratio: ~0.12x (well within 0.3x maximum) - Massive upcoming capex (₹32,700 Cr plan) will likely require debt financing at scale in FY27–31. Filter 1.3 Verdict: PASS (currently) — flag for future cycles as capex scales

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer - FY2022: Joint venture with Thriveni group (MDO capacity addition) - FY2023: Acquired 83% stake in Thriveni Earthmovers Pvt. Ltd. for ₹70 Cr - June 2026: Board approved investment in Loka Metals Private Limited (new Telangana-based steel distribution and cut-and-bend company) - ₹32,700 Cr self-funded capex plan includes multiple new plants in parallel

The company is running an aggressive expansion playbook involving acquisitions AND large self-capex simultaneously. The Darwin framework is categorical: "If a business is a serial acquirer, we stay away. We can't price the risk." While each individual transaction appears justified operationally, the pattern of making acquisitions (Thriveni, Loka Metals) alongside giant capex commitments is an accumulating risk.

Filter 1.4 Verdict: FLAG — serial acquisition pattern combined with massive self-capex; framework warns to stay away

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change Iron ore mining is a slow-changing industry. The underlying demand driver (India's steel consumption) is structurally growing. However, mining lease regulation, environmental compliance, and Naxal-area risk are persistent non-market risks. Filter 1.5 Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.6 — Ownership / Alignment Promoter is the dominant shareholder (63.73%). Private — not government. Filter 1.6 Verdict: PASS

Gate 1 Summary: CONDITIONAL — Filter 1.1 governance flags + Filter 1.4 serial acquisition pattern are material concerns. Framework discipline would lean toward caution.

2. GRAVITA INDIA LTD

CMP: ₹1,714.70 | PE: 33.41x | Market Cap: ₹12,656 Cr | ROCE: 17.02% (5yr avg)

Business Description

Established in 1992 by first-generation entrepreneur Rajat Agrawal, Gravita is India's largest organized lead recycler, processing battery scrap into pure lead, lead alloys, lead oxide and related products. Operations span 70+ countries with 33 own sourcing yards and 1,900+ touchpoints. Revenue is split ~88% lead, ~12% aluminium/plastic/turnkey. Under "Vision 2029," Gravita is diversifying into rubber recycling, lithium-ion recycling, and aluminium, targeting 30%+ non-lead revenue by FY29. Installed capacity expanded from 3.40 lakh MT to targeted 7 lakh MT+ by FY28 (₹1,200 Cr ongoing capex). Promoter Rajat Agrawal holds ~55.9% (reduced from ~73% over 3 years via QIP/dilution).

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.2: Not a turnaround. Business is profitable and growing. PASS

Filter 1.3: - D/E ~0.2x (comfortable within 0.3x maximum) - FCF positive across 5yr (₹106 Cr), 3yr (₹74 Cr), and previous annual (₹175 Cr) - Working capital intensive (78-day cycle) but FCF generation is improving. PASS

Filter 1.4: No significant acquisitions. Growth is organic. PASS

Filter 1.5: Lead recycling is a slow-changing industry. Regulatory tailwinds (Battery Waste Management Rules 2022, EPR mandates) actually favour organized players. Long-term risk: lithium-ion batteries in EVs reduce lead-acid demand over 10-15 years, but this is a slow transition with 2-wheelers, telecom, and industrial UPS remaining lead-acid-driven for the foreseeable future. PASS
