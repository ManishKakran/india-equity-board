INDUSTRY OVERVIEW & OUTSIDE VIEW (Convergence Check)

Before analysing any individual company, the Darwin framework demands an outside/convergence view of the industry: "Where else has this worked? Can companies in this industry structurally earn returns above cost of capital?"

What "Forest Products" Means in This List

The three companies span two very different sub-industries within the BSE "Forest Products" classification:

Sub-Industry A — Katha/Cutch (Forest Extracts) Indian Wood Products manufactures Katha and Cutch from the heartwood of Acacia catechu (Khair tree). Katha is an essential ingredient in paan/paan masala; Cutch is used in leather tanning, dyeing, and paper industries. This is a centuries-old cottage-and-factory industry, highly fragmented, dominated by unorganised small producers.

Sub-Industry B — Plywood, Panels & Laminates Pratik Panels and National Plywood operate in the plywood/panel space. India is the world's third-largest consumer and second-largest producer of plywood, with consumption volumes of approximately 12 million cubic meters annually. The India plywood market was valued at ~INR 247.85 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow at a CAGR of ~5–7% through 2034, driven by urbanisation, residential construction, and modular furniture demand.

Industry Structure Assessment

Katha/Cutch sub-industry: - Deeply fragmented; dominated by cottage-scale unorganised producers - Raw material (Khair wood) increasingly constrained by forest regulations and depletion - Demand linked to paan consumption — a slow-to-declining cultural habit in urban India - No pricing power; commodity product with no brand differentiation - Low barriers to entry; technology publicly available (Central Forest Research Institute) - Outside view verdict: Industry structurally incapable of sustained high ROCE. Most players in this space earn sub-10% returns. No structural moat exists.

Plywood/Panels sub-industry: - Highly fragmented; organised sector holds ~62% share (2025), rest is unorganised - Persistent raw material volatility (timber availability, import dependence) and intense price competition - Strong tailwinds from urbanisation, housing schemes (PM Awas Yojana), and commercial construction - Competitive landscape dominated by Century Plyboards and Greenply; smaller players lack pricing power - Subject to environmental regulations, illegal logging concerns, and import competition - Outside view verdict: Industry allows some players (branded, organised) to earn above cost of capital, but most small players cannot. The structural advantage belongs to scale + brand + distribution, none of which apply to Pratik Panels or National Plywood at their current size.

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter / Management Integrity

The company was founded in 1919; current promoters acquired control in 1980 (K K Mohta family). No publicly available records of SEBI/NCLT regulatory censure, fraud, or court actions. Promoter shareholding appears stable. No government ownership.

Verdict: PASS — No integrity red flags found. However, note that management has presided over decades of sub-cost-of-capital returns without structural improvement.

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check

ROCE of 3.32% has been persistently below any reasonable cost of capital (10–12%) for multiple years. ROE 5-year average is a negligible 0.70%. Net profit fell -39% YoY in Q4 FY25. There is no evidence of a structural fix underway — no new product lines, no capacity expansion communicated.

Any investment thesis here would be predicated on a turnaround: either a recovery in Katha demand, an improvement in raw material access, or a management-driven restructuring. None of these are demonstrated historical facts.

Verdict: DISQUALIFY — Filter 1.2 fails. The investment thesis is entirely dependent on a turnaround that has not materialised over decades. The book is explicit: "For every Tesla and Eicher, hundreds of turnaround stories are consigned to the dustbin of history."

INDUSTRY NOTE FOR FUTURE SCREENING

If seeking forest products / wood-based materials exposure within the Darwin framework, the only companies in India that have demonstrated sustained ROCE >20% with promoter-led governance are Century Plyboards (India) Ltd. and Greenpanel Industries Ltd. — both in the organised plywood/MDF segment. These are not part of this BSE sector grouping but represent the industry's quality tier. They would still need to pass all six Gate 1 filters and be assessed at the right price before any action.

The broader lesson from this sector scan: industries dominated by unorganised competition, commodity pricing, raw material dependence on forest access, and regulatory risk (forest laws, timber import rules) make it structurally very difficult for any company to sustain ROCE >20% over a decade. The outside/convergence view strongly cautions against this sector as a hunting ground for permanent-ownership quality businesses.
