📊 INDUSTRY OVERVIEW — Indian Listed Education Sector

Macro Context (Latest Data)

India's education market is projected to reach US$313 billion by FY30. The Indian edtech market is expected to grow nearly fourfold to approximately US$30 billion by 2031, driven by rising digital adoption, affordable mobile data, and growing internet penetration. The school market alone reached US$54.2 billion in 2024 and is expected to reach US$135.6 billion by 2033 (CAGR ~10.2%).

2025 was a year of stabilisation, not speculation. Roughly 85 education transactions were recorded during the year, with just over $580 million of capital raised — below historical highs but with improving quality. Investors moved away from underwriting "category creation" stories and toward underwriting execution, governance, and durability. Capital increasingly gravitated toward real assets: schools, financing platforms, professional education, and regulated institutions.

2026 Outlook: Consolidation is expected to accelerate across fragmented sub-sectors. Regulatory clarity and governance will increasingly influence valuations. AI is deepening its role across delivery and operations. The correction phase is over — what follows is a more mature, institution-led phase.

Key Industry Metrics (Sector Benchmarks)

Metric Benchmark
Student enrollment growth (healthy) >12% annually
Revenue per student growth (pricing power) >8% annually
EBITDA margin — coaching/K-12 >15%
EBITDA margin — corporate skilling >20%
Corporate contract renewal rate (B2B stickiness) >80%

Convergence Test (Outside View) — Darwin Filter

Per the Darwin framework: "If most companies in the industry can't hack it, why should I believe that this company is unique?"

Verdict on Indian Listed Education Industry: This is a Type 3 convergence industry — only select companies earn returns above cost of capital. The majority of the 28 listed companies have negative FCF, negative or near-zero ROCE, or both. The industry as a whole does NOT structurally deliver value. Therefore, any investment must be in the undisputed sub-sector leader with demonstrated ROCE > 20% over 5–10 years.

The government's Skill India Programme continuation (₹8,800 crore outlay till 2026) provides structural tailwind specifically for corporate and vocational training businesses.
