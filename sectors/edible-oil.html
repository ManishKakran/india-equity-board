PART I — INDUSTRY DEEP DIVE (Outside View / Convergence Test)

Before individual stock analysis, Pulak Prasad mandates understanding the industry-level ROCE. If the median industry ROCE is structurally below 12–15%, individual stock selection becomes far harder to justify.

1.1 Industry Overview

India is the world's second-largest edible oil consumer, with annual consumption of 25–26 million tonnes (MMT) in 2025-26. The sector is both enormous and structurally fragile.

Key structural facts (2025-26): - Market volume: 25.33 MMT (2025), projected at 28.34 MMT by 2034 — a modest CAGR of just 1.26% - Domestic production covers only 40–45% of demand; imports bridge the remaining 55–60% - Annual import bill: approximately ₹1–1.3 lakh crore - Palm oil dominates with 38% market share by volume, followed by soybean oil (~20%) and sunflower oil (~12%) - The market is pivoting from loose/unbranded oil toward packaged, branded, and fortified products — a structural tailwind for companies with strong brands

Darwin Verdict on the Industry:
This is a slow-changing, essential-goods industry — it passes Filter 1.5 (industry pace of change). Cooking oil demand is millennia-old; no technological disruption is plausible. However, profitability is structurally constrained by commodity pass-through pricing, import dependency, and government tariff intervention.

1.2 The Structural Vulnerability: Import Dependence

India imports ~57% of its edible oil requirement from Indonesia, Malaysia, Argentina, and Ukraine. This creates three compounding risks:

  1. Global price volatility risk — CPO (crude palm oil) prices, soybean oil prices, and sunflower oil prices fluctuate sharply due to weather, geopolitics, and logistics
  2. Government tariff intervention risk — The government cut BCD on crude edible oils from 20% to 10% in May 2025, and again in June 2025, forcing companies to immediately pass savings to consumers. Companies cannot enjoy cost benefits for long.
  3. Rupee depreciation risk — A 5% INR depreciation directly inflates import costs. Imported inflation spiked to 31% in February 2025 before easing.

Bottom line: Any business that relies primarily on trading/refining of imported crude oil operates in a structurally thin-margin, government-controlled environment. Brand and distribution are the only durable moats.

PART V — INDUSTRY INVESTMENT FRAMEWORK CONCLUSIONS

What This Sector Is (Darwinian "Outside View")

The Indian edible oil industry, viewed from the outside, is a structurally challenged commodity business with pockets of genuine quality:

  • Median ROCE for all 37 listed companies: ~10–12% — barely above cost of capital
  • The industry is deeply import-dependent (57%) and subject to frequent government tariff intervention
  • Price competition is intense; margins are thin for commodity refiners
  • Government policy acts as an invisible hand — cutting duties when prices rise, raising them when imports threaten domestic farmers — making sustainable margin expansion difficult

The Exceptions That Survive

Within this difficult sector, two types of companies earn durable above-average returns:

  1. Branded FMCG players (Marico) — Brand moat allows premium pricing; edible oil is a gateway to a broader personal care/wellness portfolio. The oil business cross-subsidises brand building. ROCE stays structurally high because capital intensity is low relative to brand value.

  2. Efficient large-scale processors with growing proprietary brands (Gokul Agro) — Scale advantages in refining + branded distribution give an edge. However, these companies are more exposed to commodity cycles and government policy than pure FMCG players.

What the Framework Says to Do

Action Condition
Buy Marico Only if PE drops to 35–40x (requires ~40% correction from CMP)
Buy Gokul Agro If debt/equity drops to ≤0.3, FCF stays positive, and PE stays below 18x — accumulate below ₹180
Research Kriti Nutrients / Ambar Protein Conduct deep due diligence on management, promoter integrity, and business moat before allocating
Avoid the rest Commodity refiners with no brand moat and inconsistent FCF are capital traps

PART VI — KEY RISKS TO MONITOR (2025-2026 Context)

  1. Global palm oil price volatility — CPO prices are driven by Indonesian/Malaysian weather, export policies, and biofuel mandates. A sharp price spike will compress margins of all refiners instantly.

  2. Government duty intervention — The May-June 2025 BCD cuts set a precedent. Whenever consumer inflation rises, the government will reduce import duties and force pass-through — eliminating short-term margin benefits.

  3. NMEO-OP execution disappointment — Progress on oil palm cultivation is significantly below target. India will remain import-dependent for at least 10-15 years despite policy ambition.

  4. Rupee depreciation — A weaker INR directly inflates the cost of the ~57% of edible oils that India imports. This is a recurring systemic risk.

  5. Patanjali group's market aggression — Patanjali Foods is using government-backed oil palm expansion and the Ramdev brand to build scale rapidly. This pressures all branded players including Marico's Saffola.

  6. Consolidation wave — Gokul Agro's acquisition spree signals a consolidation phase. Smaller players will face increasing competitive pressure on both volume and pricing.
