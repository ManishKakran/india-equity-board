PART A — INDUSTRY ANALYSIS & OUTSIDE VIEW

"One of our principles of convergence investing is that if the industry allows its companies to make money consistently, we love it; if not, we better have a perfect reason for spending even one minute analysing a business." — Pulak Prasad

A1. Industry Size & Growth Trajectory

India's gems and jewellery sector is one of the largest in the world. Key facts as of June 2026:

  • Domestic market size: ~₹7,31,255 crore (~USD 85 billion) as of January 2026; projected to grow to ~USD 130 billion by 2030 (IBEF, 2026).
  • Long-run CAGR: Multiple forecasters (IMARC, Grand View, Technavio) project 5–9% CAGR through 2033–34, driven by rising disposable incomes, urbanisation, wedding demand, and a cultural affinity for gold as both adornment and store of value.
  • India's global position: India accounted for ~24% of global jewellery market revenue in 2024 and is expected to be the global leader by 2033.
  • Exports: Gems & jewellery exports in FY26 (April–February) stood at USD 25.93 billion.
  • Organisation shift: The top 5 listed players hold only ~15% of the total market — the vast majority (~85%) remains unorganised. Organised players are gaining share structurally through mandatory hallmarking, GST compliance, and consumer preference for certified, branded jewellery.

A2. Critical Near-Term Disruption (June 2026 — Active Risk)

⚠️ Gold Import Duty Hike — May 13, 2026 (HIGHEST CURRENT RISK)

The government raised gold import duty from 6% to 15% effective May 13, 2026 — the steepest single hike on record and a reversal of the July 2024 reduction. This was accompanied by PM Modi's public appeal to citizens to reduce gold purchases for a year, citing a record gold import bill of USD 72 billion in FY26 and pressure on the INR (depreciated >7% YTD).

Immediate impact: - Jewellery stocks fell 6–12% on May 11–13, 2026. - IBJA reported a ~70% drop in near-term gold demand in the fortnight ended May 27, 2026. - WGC projects a 50–60 tonne (~10% y/y) decline in India's combined jewellery and bar-and-coin demand in 2026. - The unorganised sector is worst affected; organised players benefit relatively from exchange programmes and transparent sourcing. - Historical precedent (2013, 2022 duty hikes) shows smuggling rises with higher duties, creating market distortion.

Darwin Framework Assessment of this Disruption: This is a proximate cause (policy shock), not an ultimate cause (permanent destruction of the jewellery business model in India). Cultural demand for gold is deeply embedded. The organised-to-unorganised shift is a structural multi-decade story unaffected by a cyclical duty hike. However, near-term earnings of all jewellery companies will be negatively impacted — creating potential entry opportunity for high-quality businesses if valuations correct to the 14.9x zone.

Convergence Verdict: This is a Type 3 industry — only some companies make money consistently. The industry does NOT structurally guarantee returns above cost of capital. Most players (especially smaller jewellers, diamond exporters, and watch companies below Titan) have mediocre economics. The Darwin framework demands that any investment in this space must be the undisputed leader or a company with demonstrated and durable structural advantages.

Key industry-level structural challenges: 1. Working capital intensity: Jewellery is inherently inventory-heavy. Gold inventory ties up large capital, making FCF chronically negative for expanding players even when profitable. 2. Gold price pass-through: Revenue looks high but margins are thin; raw material (gold) is a commodity. ROCE compression risk is real in high-price environments. 3. Franchise/trust dependency: Brand trust takes decades to build but can erode quickly (fraud, hallmarking failures). Several small-cap companies in this list have no brand equity. 4. Fragmented competition: 85% unorganised market means pricing discipline is weak. 5. Regulatory risk: Hallmarking, GST, import duties — this sector faces recurring government intervention on gold policy.

A4. Industry Structural Tailwinds (Long-term)

  1. Organised sector's share growing from ~15% to potentially 30–35% over the next decade — massive runway for organised leaders.
  2. Lab-grown diamonds creating an affordable luxury growth segment (BlueStone invested ₹25 crore in Ethera lab-grown diamond brand, Feb 2026).
  3. India-UK CETA (signed July 2025) eliminated 2.5–4% import duties on plain gold and diamond jewellery, boosting exports.
  4. Female workforce participation rising → women's self-purchase jewellery (non-wedding) growing.
  5. Digital and omnichannel distribution opening up Tier 2–3 cities.

🔬 FULL DARWIN ANALYSIS — COMPANY 1: TITAN COMPANY (CMP: ₹4,024.60)

Market Cap: ₹3,57,298 Cr | P/E: 69.4x | ROCE: 25.8% | ROE 5Yr: 32.45% | ROE: 37.68%

GATE 1 — AVOID BIG RISKS

Filter 1.1 — Promoter / Management Integrity: Titan is a Tata Group company (Tata Sons ~53% stake). The Tata Group has one of India's most impeccable governance records. No history of fraud, SEBI censure, or related-party abuse. The Tata name is synonymous with ethical corporate practice in India. However, the controlling shareholder is Tata Sons (a diversified conglomerate), not a founder-entrepreneur. Titan itself is operationally run by professional management (C.K. Venkataraman as MD). The book explicitly states Nalanda invests "almost exclusively in businesses owned and run by entrepreneurs where the entrepreneur is typically the largest shareholder." Tata Sons is institutional, not entrepreneurial. This is a borderline case — not a government-owned enterprise, but also not a classic founder-promoter structure. Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS — governance is impeccable; ownership structure is institutional (not founder-led), which is a partial flag under Filter 1.6.

Filter 1.2 — Turnaround Check: Titan is NOT a turnaround story. Tanishq, Titan watches, Taneira, CaratLane — all are growing, profitable businesses. No restructuring dependency. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.3 — Debt/Leverage: Screener D/E data shows Titan is largely funded by lease liabilities (Ind AS 116 treatment). Per simplywall.st, total debt ₹181–208B but this includes lease obligations for 2,500+ retail stores. Operational ROCE of 25.8% and interest coverage ~5–8x per recent data. The FCF is negative in FY25 (₹-1,011 Cr for FY25) — a concern. D/E when adjusted for leases is materially higher than 0.3. Under strict Darwin criteria (D/E ≤ 0.3, cash > debt), Titan does not pass this filter cleanly. Verdict: CONDITIONAL FAIL — significant lease-adjusted debt; negative FCF in FY25; does not meet strict D/E ≤ 0.3 criterion.

Filter 1.4 — Serial Acquirer: Titan acquired CaratLane (online diamond jewellery) in stages, recently buying out Tiger Global's stake. It has also expanded into Taneira (ethnic wear) and other categories. While not a reckless acquirer, it has shown a pattern of adjacent-market acquisitions. The CaratLane bet has been strategically sound so far, but the pattern of diversification (watches → jewellery → eyewear → ethnic wear → online) makes this a semi-conglomerate in nature. Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS — not a serial acquirer in the destructive M&A sense; CaratLane integration appears value-accretive.

Filter 1.5 — Industry Pace of Change: Jewellery demand in India is deeply cultural and slow-changing at the core. The watch segment is more susceptible to smartwatch disruption, but this is a smaller revenue contributor. Overall, the industry's core (gold jewellery) is genuinely slow-changing. Verdict: PASS

Filter 1.6 — Ownership / Alignment: Tata Sons is the dominant shareholder. Not government-owned. Not a founder-promoter. Tata Group's long-term institutional alignment with business quality is strong, but not the classic Nalanda pattern. Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS

Gate 1 Overall: Multiple conditional flags (debt/lease, non-founder ownership, negative FCF). Under strict Darwin criteria, Titan would face scrutiny on Filter 1.3. A pragmatic read: Titan's lease liabilities are operational (not financial leverage), Tata governance is excellent, and the business is not a turnaround. GATE 1: MARGINAL PASS with debt/FCF caveat.

GATE 2 — BUY HIGH QUALITY AT A FAIR PRICE

Analysis 2.1 — Historical ROCE: - Current ROCE: 25.8% — above 20% Darwin minimum. - 5-year pattern: ROCE was ~40% in FY23, compressed to ~25% in FY25 partly due to Ind AS lease accounting and capital intensity of rapid store expansion. - Q3 FY26 jewellery segment grew ~41% YoY; FY26 topline crossed ₹75,000 crore. - Trend: ROCE is declining from peak (40%+) toward the 20–25% zone. This is a structural concern under Darwin criteria. Verdict: PASS (barely — current ROCE at 25.8%, declining from much higher levels)

Analysis 2.2 — Robustness: - Balance Sheet: Negative FCF in FY25; significant lease-adjusted debt. Moderately weak. - Competitive Advantage: Tanishq is India's most trusted jewellery brand with 35%+ premium market share. Legitimate moat built over 30 years. CaratLane is a strong digital moat. Brand + trust + experience = genuine and durable advantage. - Customer Fragmentation: Highly fragmented retail customers. No concentration risk. - Management Stability: C.K. Venkataraman has steered the company well; Tata governance is consistent. - Industry: Type 3 (only leaders make money) — Titan IS the undisputed leader. Verdict: MODERATE-TO-HIGH ROBUSTNESS — brand moat is genuine; financial robustness is weaker (FCF, lease debt).

Analysis 2.3 — Management Quality: - Tanishq gold exchange programme is a brilliant capital allocation move — reduces gold inventory risk, maintains customer engagement. - FY26 revenue crossing ₹75,000 crore shows consistent execution. - Management is honest about headwinds; disciplined margin management. - No catastrophic acquisitions; CaratLane appears well-managed. Verdict: EXCELLENT — operational execution and capital allocation discipline are strong.

Analysis 2.4 — Valuation: - Current P/E: 69.4x TTM - Current Sensex TTM PE: ~22–23x (approximate, June 2026) - Nalanda target entry: 14.9x (25–30% below Sensex PE) - Required entry PE for Darwin zone: ~15–17x - Current PE of 69.4x is 4x above the Darwin entry zone — severely expensive by Darwin criteria. Verdict: FAIL — price is extremely far from Darwin entry zone.

Analysis 2.5 — Proximate vs. Ultimate Cause: The May 2026 gold duty hike and stock correction (~8%) is a proximate cause. Titan's ultimate business quality (brand, franchise, Tata governance) is intact. But even after the correction, PE at 69x is nowhere near Darwin entry levels. Verdict: PROXIMATE FEAR ONLY — but price gap to entry zone is vast.
