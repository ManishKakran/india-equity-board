🏭 Industry Overview

India's Castings & Forgings sector is a foundational pillar of the country's manufacturing economy, producing critical components for automotive, aerospace, railways, defence, mining, cement, and infrastructure. India is the world's third-largest casting producer, and the sector contributes approximately ₹50,000 crore to the economy, with exports accounting for over 25% of total production.

Market Size & Growth (Latest Data, 2026): - India Foundry Market: USD 28.72 billion in 2026, projected to reach USD 46.72 billion by 2031 at a 10.22% CAGR (Mordor Intelligence, Feb 2026) - India Foundry & Casting Market: USD 22.0 billion in 2025, projected to reach USD 57.9 billion by 2034 at a 10.78% CAGR (IMARC Group) - Metal Forging Market: Expected to grow from USD 5.08 billion (2023) to USD 9.75 billion by 2030 at a 9.8% CAGR - Investment Casting (niche): 5.77% CAGR from 2025–2035, driven by aerospace & automotive demand

🚀 Structural Tailwinds (Ultimate Causes for Optimism)

  1. Infrastructure Supercycle: Government capex on roads, railways, ports, and smart cities creates sustained domestic demand for castings and forgings.
  2. Automotive Growth + EV Transition: India's auto-component sector reached ₹6.73 lakh crore (USD 80.2 billion) in FY25, up 9.6% YoY. EV adoption (domestic production crossed 1 million units in FY26) is driving high demand for aluminium HPDC components. Jaya Hind Industries installed India's largest 4,400-ton HPDC machine at Pune in Dec 2024 to serve this market.
  3. China+1 Global Sourcing Shift: US tariffs of 100–125% on Chinese EVs and 50% on lithium-ion batteries are redirecting supply chains toward India.
  4. Safeguard Duties on Chinese Iron Castings: India imposed 40% safeguard duties on Chinese iron castings (effective 2025–26), redirecting orders to domestic manufacturers and compressing lead times for Indian foundries.
  5. Aerospace & Defence: PTC Industries (Aerolloy Technologies) developed India's first advanced casting technology for Single Crystal and Directionally Solidified turbine blades in June 2024 — a landmark for domestic aerospace manufacturing.
  6. Make in India / PLI Tailwind: Policy push to raise manufacturing to 25% of GDP supports the entire sector.

⚠️ Structural Headwinds & Risks (Key Risks to Monitor)

  1. CBAM (EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism): Fully effective from January 2026 — a 38.8% effective levy on castings above 1.5 tonnes CO₂/tonne entering Europe. Kolhapur exporters (typically emitting 1.8–2.2 t CO₂/t) face immediate margin compression. ~70% of Indian foundries are MSMEs with limited capital to absorb compliance costs. 8–12 micro-foundries have been shutting per quarter since mid-2025, accelerating sector consolidation. Large, listed players are the beneficiaries of this consolidation.
  2. Raw Material Volatility: India slashed coke import quotas in H1 2025, driving petcoke prices from ~USD 360/t to ~USD 480/t. Scrap and energy price volatility remains a persistent margin risk.
  3. EV Disruption — Double-Edged Sword: EVs use far fewer forged/cast components per vehicle than ICE (fewer moving parts, no crankshafts, camshafts etc.). Companies heavily exposed to ICE powertrain components face structural volume risk over a 10-year horizon.
  4. China Import Pressure: Despite safeguard duties on iron castings, Chinese competition remains a threat at the lower end. Chinese foundries now match European precision standards.
  5. Automotive Softening (2025): The sector broadly declined ~20% over FY25 due to softening demand in automotive and capital goods, elevated raw material costs, and import competition.
  6. Commoditisation Risk: Lower-grade, commodity castings face perpetual price pressure. Companies with specialised, engineered products (AIA Engineering's high-chrome mill internals; aerospace-grade castings) are structurally protected.

PART 6: KEY SECTOR THEMES TO MONITOR (2026 Onwards)

  1. CBAM Beneficiaries: Large, compliant, net-cash Indian foundries will gain market share as MSMEs exit. AIA Engineering, Steelcast, and Balu Forge are best positioned.

  2. Mining Supercycle: Copper, lithium, and gold mining capex is accelerating globally for the energy transition — directly benefiting grinding media (AIA) and off-highway castings (Steelcast).

  3. EV Transition Watch: Monitor which companies have revenue exposure to ICE powertrain components (crankshafts, camshafts — high risk) vs. non-ICE components (structural castings, off-highway, defence, railways — lower risk).

  4. Safeguard Duties on Chinese Castings: 40% duty (2025–26) is a direct near-term tailwind for domestic foundries. Monitor renewal/extension.

  5. Defence & Aerospace Localisation: PTC Industries' aerospace casting breakthrough (2024) signals a long-term secular opportunity for precision foundries with the right capabilities.

  6. Petcoke / Energy Cost Volatility: A persistent risk for foundries with cupola furnaces. Companies investing in electric induction furnaces or green hydrogen early will have structural cost advantage by 2030.
