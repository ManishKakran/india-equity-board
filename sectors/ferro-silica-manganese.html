INDUSTRY OVERVIEW & LATEST UPDATE (2025–2026)

What the Industry Does

Ferro alloys — ferro manganese (FeMn), silico manganese (SiMn), and ferro chrome (FeCr) — are essential inputs in steelmaking. They act as deoxidisers and alloying agents that strengthen steel, control carbon content, and improve workability. Without them, modern steel production is impossible. Approximately 90%+ of global ferro alloy consumption is tied directly to steel output.

Global Market Size & Growth

  • The silico manganese market was valued at ~USD 30 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow at a ~9% CAGR to reach USD 65.3 billion by 2034, driven by construction, automotive, infrastructure, and EV-related high-strength steel demand.
  • The ferro manganese market was valued at ~USD 17.3 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach USD 22.5 billion by 2030 at a 5.5% CAGR.
  • Asia-Pacific — principally China and India — dominates both production and consumption, accounting for approximately 75%+ of global volumes.

India-Specific Dynamics

  • India's ferro manganese market generated ~USD 379 million in revenue in 2024 and is expected to reach USD 558 million by 2030 (6.6% CAGR).
  • India possesses substantial domestic manganese ore reserves, giving its ferroalloy producers a structural cost advantage over peers who must import ore.
  • The domestic market features a mix of large integrated producers (with captive mines and power) and numerous smaller, non-integrated players — creating a wide quality dispersion.
  • "Make in India" and infrastructure investment programmes are structural tailwinds for domestic steel demand and, therefore, ferro alloy consumption.
  • India exported approximately 1.67 million tonnes of manganese alloys in the year to October 2025 and 1.83 million tonnes in 2024.

Critical Industry Headwinds (2025–2026) — Highly Material

  1. EU Safeguard Quotas (Effective 2026): Europe has capped India's ferro-alloy imports for 2026 at 126,800 tonnes of silico-manganese (price threshold €1,392/t) and 69,900 tonnes of ferro-manganese (threshold €1,316/t). These quotas cut India's potential EU shipments by nearly half. Europe typically accounts for ~40% of India's ferro-alloy exports, meaning 300,000–400,000 tonnes may need to find alternative markets. Indian 60% silico-manganese export prices fell from $840–860/t to $780–800/t fob in December 2025 on this news.
  2. EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM, fully effective January 2026): Covers ferro-manganese and ferro-chrome. European importers must now pay charges based on embedded carbon emissions. With ferro-manganese at ~2.07 tCO₂/tonne and EU carbon prices around €80/tCO₂, the extra cost is roughly €165/t — adding 10–15% to landed European prices. This substantially erodes competitiveness of Indian exports to Europe.
  3. Price Environment: Silico-manganese prices in India in Q1 2026 were marginally firmer (+0.55% vs Q4 2025) but remain under pressure from overcapacity and constrained export demand. The highly competitive domestic market with significant installed capacity constrains price upside.
  4. Manganese Ore Supply: Pricing and supply remain closely tied to manganese ore availability from Africa and South America, creating raw material volatility for non-integrated producers.
  5. Energy Costs: Power is a major cost item in smelting. Companies without captive power plants are structurally disadvantaged.
  6. Low Technical Barriers: Production of ferro manganese and silico manganese has relatively low technical barriers, leading to geographic dispersion of capacity and intense price competition. Market concentration is low — the top players globally hold only ~18% combined share.

Industry Convergence Assessment (Nalanda Framework)

The convergence/outside-view test asks: does this industry structurally allow its companies to earn returns above the cost of capital?

Looking at the screener data, only 2 of 13 companies have ROCE >20%. Seven companies have negative ROCE. This is consistent with a commodity-driven, cyclical industry with low technical moats, intense competition, price-taking behaviour, energy intensity, and raw material dependence. The industry's structural verdict: Category (3) — Only some companies make money — must be the undisputed leader with deep integration advantages to consider.

Balance Sheet: Cash > Debt. FCF consistently positive over 5 years. Debt/equity is minimal. Balance sheet is robust.

Customer Base: 90%+ of output is exported (Korea, Taiwan, Japan, Europe). Export concentration in Europe is now a headwind given EU safeguard quotas, though IMFA plans to shift to 60–70% export / 30–40% domestic post-expansion. Customer concentration within segments appears reasonable, but geographic export concentration (Europe) is a risk.

Management Stability: Panda family has managed the company for decades. The Tata Steel acquisition, funded internally, demonstrates capital allocation discipline.

Industry Attractiveness: Ferro chrome for stainless steel is structurally attractive for integrated low-cost producers but commoditised for non-integrated ones. IMFA's integration makes it a value-creating outlier in an otherwise ordinary industry.

Verdict: MODERATE ROBUSTNESS — the integration advantage is real, but commodity price exposure, export concentration risk (EU quotas/CBAM), and ROCE near the Nalanda minimum prevent a HIGH ROBUSTNESS rating.

INDUSTRY FRAMEWORK CONCLUSIONS

  1. This is a value-destroying industry for most participants: 7 of 13 companies have negative ROCE. Only 2 clear the 20% Nalanda minimum on current data. The Nalanda convergence test's Category (3) applies: "Only some companies make money — must be the undisputed leader to consider."

  2. Integration is the only durable moat: Companies with captive ore mines + captive power (IMFA, partially Maithan) consistently outperform. Non-integrated players are price-takers in a commodity market with structurally inadequate returns.

  3. Export headwinds are material and multi-year: EU safeguard quotas (2026) + CBAM effectively close ~half of India's traditional European ferro-alloy export market. The 300,000–400,000 tonnes that cannot enter Europe must find alternative buyers (Asian steel mills, domestic) at potentially lower realisations. This is a sector-level proximate headwind — severe but not necessarily permanent.

  4. Domestic steel growth is the structural tailwind: India's long-term steel consumption growth, infrastructure investment, and EV/automotive sector expansion create a multi-decade demand floor for ferro alloys. "Make in India" provides secular support.

  5. Price volatility is endemic: Ferro alloy prices have historically fluctuated 30–50% peak-to-trough. Only companies with cost advantages (integration, captive power, scale) can reliably earn above cost of capital across a full cycle.
