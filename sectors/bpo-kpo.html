PART I: INDUSTRY OUTSIDE VIEW (Convergence Test)

Before picking any individual stock, Pulak Prasad insists on understanding industry-level economics first. A great company in a structurally broken industry will rarely survive long enough to compound.

Industry Overview: BPO / KPO in India (2025–2026)

Market Size & Growth India's BPO sector was valued at ~$49.87 billion in 2025 (domestic-only definition; IT+BPO total is larger) and is projected to reach $139.35 billion by 2033, implying a 12.64% CAGR. The global BPO market reached $300 billion in 2024 and is projected at $525 billion by 2030. India commands roughly 10% of the global IT services market and is the world's dominant outsourcing delivery hub — over 35% of global banking/finance companies outsource processes to India.

Revenue Mix: Voice-based services ~35%, Non-voice (chat/email) ~45%, KPO ~20%.

🚨 Industry Risk: The AI/Automation Disruption Factor (FILTER 1.5)

This is the single most important structural risk for this sector. Key data points:

  • Prominent voices (Vinod Khosla, February 2026) warn India's BPO/IT services could "almost completely disappear" within five years as AI systems rapidly outperform human expertise.
  • AI chatbots and virtual agents already handle 45% of non-voice interactions (email/chat), reducing Average Handling Time by 20%.
  • AI startup Decagon reports 80% customer service resolution rates; Juniper AI reports 80% fewer insurance claim denials and 50% faster processing vs. human BPO workers.
  • Generative AI startups (a16z thesis, early 2025) are explicitly targeting the $300 billion BPO industry for disruption.
  • Major BPO firms are responding: Wipro reported 140% increase in AI adoption across projects; Infosys deployed 100+ AI agents.
  • Volume-based, voice-heavy, and repetitive-task BPOs face structural headcount reduction. KPO firms with deep domain expertise and switching costs face lower but still meaningful disruption risk.

Darwin Verdict on Industry Pace of Change: 🔴 HIGH DISRUPTION RISK for commoditized BPO. MODERATE for high-value KPO with domain moats. The industry is NOT in the "slow-changing" category that Nalanda prefers.

This alone disqualifies most companies in this sector under Filter 1.5.

However, the framework allows nuance: firms that are evolving into AI-orchestrators (embedding AI into delivery, transitioning from labor-arbitrage to intellectual capital) may survive the punctuation event. We apply this judgment selectively.

PART IV: KEY RISKS TO MONITOR (For ALL BPO/KPO Holdings)

1. AI/GenAI Structural Disruption (Primary Risk) The next 24–36 months will be decisive. Watch for: percentage of revenue from AI-augmented vs. human-labor processes; client RFPs specifying AI delivery; headcount trends relative to revenue. eClerx embedding GenAI into delivery is the right response — execution quality matters.

2. USD/GBP Currency Risk Both eClerx and Alldigi Tech earn primarily in USD/GBP, with INR costs. A strengthening rupee would compress margins. Monitor RBI policy and INR trajectory.

3. Client Concentration KPO firms with Fortune 2000 clients tend to have deep but concentrated relationships. Any large client loss would be material.

4. Promoter Track Record (Alldigi Tech specific) Digitide Solutions became promoter only in March 2025. Two annual report cycles (FY26 and FY27) are needed to assess capital allocation behavior, dividend policy, and whether they invest in the business or extract value.

5. Wage Inflation and Attrition India's tech/services talent market remains competitive. Wage inflation can compress margins in labor-heavy BPO operations more than in capital-intensive industries.
