THE NARRATIVE ARC

FY2019–2020: The Plateau

TCS's 19% revenue growth in FY2019 was the last year of an era. Trade tensions, Brexit, and post-demonetisation normalisation drove deceleration to 6.7% by FY2020. EBIT margins compressed to 22.9%, the company provisioned ₹1,218 Cr for Epic Systems in FY2021, and management began the strategic reset toward cloud and SBWS capabilities.

FY2021–2022: COVID as Accelerant

COVID acceleration — not deceleration — defined FY2021. SBWS proved delivery viability. Travel costs collapsed (₹2,600 Cr savings), margins spiked to 25.9%. Management placed the boldest bet in company history: hiring 103,546 net new employees in FY2022. FY2022 grew 16.8%, FY2023 at 17.6%.

FY2023–2024: The Great Stall

Three simultaneous headwinds: US banking crisis (BFSI = 37% of revenue), GenAI pause, post-surge normalisation. Revenue growth collapsed to 6.8%. Net headcount fell for the first time in listed history (–13,249). Epic fully resolved (₹958 Cr additional charge). CEO changed: Gopinathan → K Krithivasan.

FY2025: The Non-Recovery

Americas flat (–0.05%) for second consecutive year. CMT surged +16.5%. India exploded +62.7% (BSNL Phase 1). COO changed: N.G. Subramaniam retired (age 60, company policy) → Aarthi Subramanian. TCV: US$39.4 Bn. HyperVault AI data center announced. TPG $1 Bn equity partnership.

FY2026 (Q1–Q3): AI Inflection

Q3FY26 revenue: ₹67,087 Cr (+4.9% YoY). AI revenue: US$1.8 Bn annualized (+17.3% QoQ CC). Headcount: 582,163 (still falling). Invested funds rebuilt to US$7.1 Bn (~₹61,000 Cr). India wage code one-off: ₹2,128 Cr. Management: "Confident of a good CY2026."

💬 In plain English: This is the timeline of TCS's last seven years, told as a story instead of a spreadsheet. The short version: strong growth, then COVID actually helped TCS (remote work meant more companies needed its services), then a hiring binge, then a rough patch in 2023-24 when two things went wrong at once — American banks got nervous and cut spending, and companies paused AI projects to figure out what they even wanted — and TCS's growth crashed and it shrank its workforce for the first time ever. The concerning part isn't in the past; it's that FY2025 didn't really bounce back in TCS's biggest market (the US stayed flat), even though other regions like India did well. FY2026 so far looks like slow, steady improvement. Watch whether the US recovers — that's the open question this whole timeline is building toward.
